Read the full story

Noise’s story begins in Latin: nausea meant “seasickness.” A possible source, from Greek 'naus', ship: the uproar and distress of a ship's passengers. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as noise. Meant a loud disturbance or strife. It reached English by 1200s as noise. In science, 'noise' now means unwanted signal. Today, noise means a sound, especially a loud or unpleasant one.
