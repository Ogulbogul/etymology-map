Read the full story

Troubadour’s story begins in Occitan: trobar meant “to compose, find.” To compose, literally 'to find' (a poem or tune). Within Occitan, it grew into trobador by 1100s. Poets such as Guilhem IX, Duke of Aquitaine, invented the lyric of courtly love. From there it passed into French as troubadour by 1700s. Revived by French scholars of medieval literature. It reached English by 1720s as troubadour. Borrowed as interest in medieval poetry grew. Today, troubadour means a medieval lyric poet of southern France; a singer of love songs.
