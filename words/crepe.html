Read the full story

Crepe’s story begins in Latin: crispus meant “curled, wrinkled.” An adjective for curled or wrinkled texture. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Old French as crespe. Applied to a ruffled, crimped fabric. From there it passed into English as crape by 1797. Borrowed for the crinkled mourning fabric. It reached English by 1877 as crepe. The same word was applied to a thin pancake for its wrinkled, lacy appearance. Today, crepe means a thin, light pancake, or a light crinkled fabric.
