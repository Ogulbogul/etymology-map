Read the full story

Demagogue’s story begins in Greek: dēmagōgos meant “popular leader.” 'leader of the people'; Athenian critics used it for populists such as Cleon, mocked by Aristophanes. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as démagogue. Taken up in political pamphlets. It reached English by 1640s as demagogue. Used from the English Civil War onward as an insult. Today, demagogue means a political leader who appeals to popular prejudice.
