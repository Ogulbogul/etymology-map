Read the full story

Humble pie’s story begins in Latin: lumbulus meant “little loin.” 'little loin', diminutive of lumbus 'loin'. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Old French as nombles. The edible innards of a deer; the l changed to n. From there it passed into English as umble pie by 1663. 'numbles' became 'umbles' by losing its n to the article; Samuel Pepys records on 8 July 1663 that Mrs Turner brought him an 'umble-pie hot out of her oven'. It reached English by 1669 as humble pie. 'humble pie' appears in print by 1669, and the pun later gave 'to eat humble pie', to be forced to apologise. Today, humble pie means (eat humble pie) to make a humble apology after being proved wrong.
