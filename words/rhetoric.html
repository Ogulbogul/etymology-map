Read the full story

Rhetoric’s story begins in Greek: rhētorikē meant “art of oratory.” Short for 'the art of the public speaker'; Aristotle wrote the defining treatise on it. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as rhetorica. One of the three core medieval school subjects with grammar and logic. It reached English by 1300s as rhetoric. Has since gained a negative sense of empty, insincere talk. Today, rhetoric means persuasive speaking or writing, often suggesting insincerity.
