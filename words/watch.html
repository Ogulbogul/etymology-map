Read the full story

Watch’s story begins in Old English: wæccan meant “to stay awake.” To stay awake or keep vigil; 'wake' comes from the same root. By 1200s-1400s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as wacche. A night watchman's shift; sailors still divide the day into 'watches'. It reached English by 1580s as watch. The portable timepiece, perhaps named for the watchman's alarm; pocket watches spread from Nuremberg soon after 1500. Today, watch means to look at attentively; a small timepiece worn or carried.
