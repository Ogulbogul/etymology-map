Read the full story

Cayenne’s story begins in Tupi: kyinha meant “a hot chili pepper.” Named a variety of hot chili pepper. By 1750s, the word had taken hold in French as Cayenne. The pepper's name was folk-associated with the French Guianese port city of Cayenne. It reached English by 1756 as cayenne. Borrowed into English as the pepper became a common spice in Atlantic trade. Today, cayenne means a hot, ground spice made from dried red chili peppers.
