Read the full story

Alabaster’s story begins in Greek: alabastros meant “a soft, pale mineral stone.” Possibly named for an Egyptian town famous for producing the soft white stone. It reached English by 1300s as alabaster. First recorded around 1384 in the Wycliffite Bible, for the alabaster jar of ointment poured over Jesus. Today, alabaster means a soft, translucent mineral, typically white, used for carving and ornaments.
