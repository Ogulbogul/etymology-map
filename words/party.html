Read the full story

Party’s story begins in Latin: pars meant “part, side.” 'part'; Caesar's Gallic War opens 'Gallia est omnis divisa in partes tres', Gaul is divided into three parts. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as partie. From partir 'to divide'; a part, a side in a quarrel, or a litigant in court, the sense still used in legal 'third party'. It reached English by 1200s as party. The political sense grew with the Whigs and Tories of the 1680s; a 'party' as a social gathering dates from the 1700s. Today, party means a social gathering; a political organisation; a person or group in an agreement.
