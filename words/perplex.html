Read the full story

Perplex’s story begins in Latin: per plicare meant “to entangle thoroughly.” To entangle thoroughly, from per- (thoroughly) and plicare (to fold). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as perplexus. Entangled, confused, from perplicare. It reached English by 1400s as perplex. Settled as the English word for confusing or puzzling someone. Today, perplex means to confuse or puzzle someone, leaving them uncertain what to think.
