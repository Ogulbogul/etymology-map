Read the full story

Identity’s story begins in Late Latin: identitas meant “sameness.” Coined by philosophers from idem 'the same', probably on the model of entitas 'being'. It reached English by 1560s as identity. Came via Middle French identité; John Locke's Essay Concerning Human Understanding (1694 edition) added a famous chapter 'Of Identity and Diversity', and Britain issued identity cards from 1939 to 1952. Today, identity means the fact of being who or what a person or thing is.
