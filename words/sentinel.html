Read the full story

Sentinel’s story begins in Latin: sentire meant “to feel, perceive.” Speculatively linked to the verb of feeling or perceiving. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as sentinella. A noun for a watchful guard, of disputed deeper formation. From there it passed into French as sentinelle by 1500s. From Italian 'sentire', to perceive, i.e. one who watches. It reached English by 1570s as sentinel. Adopted for a soldier stationed to keep watch. Today, sentinel means a soldier or guard stationed to keep watch.
