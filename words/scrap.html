Read the full story

Scrap’s story begins in Old Norse: skrap meant “scraps, trifles.” Named the leftover trifles or scraps from a meal. By late 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as scrappe. Borrowed to describe food remains after eating. It reached English by 1580s onward as scrap. Widened from food to any small leftover piece, later metal remnants. Today, scrap means a small leftover piece, or discarded material such as metal.
