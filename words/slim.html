Read the full story

Slim’s story begins in Dutch: slim meant “slanting, crooked; bad, wrong.” From Middle Dutch 'slim', meaning crooked, slanting, or morally bad. By 1650s, the word had taken hold in English as slim. Borrowed with negative senses of slyness or insignificance still attached. It reached English by 1700s onward as slim. The negative senses faded, leaving only the meaning of a slender build. Today, slim means thin or slender in build.
