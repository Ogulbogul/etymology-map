Read the full story

Contingent’s story begins in Latin: contingere meant “to touch on all sides, to happen.” To touch on all sides, to happen, from com- (together) and tangere (to touch). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as contingens. Happening by chance, dependent on circumstance, present participle of contingere. It reached English by 1300s as contingent. Settled as the English word for something dependent on circumstances, or a group representing a larger body. Today, contingent means dependent on circumstances not yet certain, or a group representing a larger body.
