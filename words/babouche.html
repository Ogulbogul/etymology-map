Read the full story

Babouche’s story begins in Persian: paposh meant “foot covering, slipper.” Compound of pa, "foot," and posh, "covering". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as babush. Adapted the Persian word, since Arabic lacks the sound "p". From there it passed into French as babouche by 1690s. From Persian 'pa', foot, plus 'push', covering. It reached English by 1690s as babouche. Entered English describing the soft Middle Eastern slipper. Today, babouche means a soft, heelless slipper of Middle Eastern or North African origin.
