Read the full story

Coherent’s story begins in Latin: haerere meant “to stick, to cling.” To stick, to cling, combined with com- (together). Within Latin, it grew into cohaerere by ancient. To stick together, from com- and haerere. By ancient, Latin speakers had reshaped it into cohaerens. Sticking together, logically connected, present participle of cohaerere. It reached English by 1500s as coherent. Settled as the English word for something logical and consistent. Today, coherent means logical and consistent, easy to understand or follow.
