Read the full story

Memsahib’s story begins in Arabic: ṣāḥib meant “companion, master.” A companion, then a master. By 1850s, the word had taken hold in Anglo-Indian as mem-sāhib. 'mem', from English 'ma'am', plus 'sahib', master. It reached English by 1850s as memsahib. Used in and about the British Raj. Today, memsahib means a European married woman in colonial India.
