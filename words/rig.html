Read the full story

Rig’s story begins in Norwegian: rigga meant “to bind, wrap.” A nautical verb for binding or wrapping, used of fitting a ship. By 1480s, the word had taken hold in English as rig. First a sailors' term for fitting a ship with masts, sails and lines. It reached English by 1700s as rig. The sense 'to manipulate dishonestly' arose from slang for a trick or swindle. Today, rig means to fit out with equipment; to manipulate an outcome dishonestly.
