Read the full story

Ashe’s story begins in Yoruba: àṣẹ meant “spiritual power or life force; also an affirmation like "amen".” Named the divine life force believed to animate gods, ancestors, and even spoken words. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as axé. Creolized in Brazil as enslaved Yoruba people carried the concept into Candomblé practice. It reached English by late 1900s as ashe. Spread into English through Santería, Candomblé, and diaspora spiritual writing as an affirming exclamation. Today, ashe means a Yoruba-derived concept of divine life force, used in English as a spiritual affirmation similar to "amen".
