Read the full story

Sirdar’s story begins in Persian: sar + -dar meant “head-holder, commander.” Compound of sar, "head," and -dar, "holder". By 1700s, the word had taken hold in Hindi/Urdu as sirdar. Used as a title for military and community leaders in South Asia. It reached English by 1800s as sirdar. Kitchener was Sirdar of the Egyptian army in the 1890s. Today, sirdar means a military commander-in-chief, or a title of leadership historically used in India, Egypt, and Sudan.
