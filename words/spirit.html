Read the full story

Spirit’s story begins in Latin: spirare meant “to breathe.” The root of 'inspire', 'expire' and 'respiration'. Within Latin, it grew into spiritus by ancient. Breath, the breath of life, the soul, from spirare. From there it passed into Old French as espirit by medieval. Carried into French for the soul or animating force of a being. It reached English by 1200s as spirit. Settled as the English word for the non-physical part of a person, or a mood. Today, spirit means the non-physical part of a person believed to give them life, or a person's mood or attitude.
