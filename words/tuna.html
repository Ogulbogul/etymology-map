Read the full story

Tuna’s story begins in Greek: thynnos meant “tunny.” The tunny fish. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as thunnus. The tunny. From there it passed into Arabic as tūn by medieval. Taken from Latin in the Mediterranean. Spanish picked it up next, as atún, around medieval. From Arabic, with the article 'al-' fused on. It reached English by 1880s as tuna. Californian fishermen took the name from Spanish; canned tuna boomed from San Diego from the 1900s. Today, tuna means a large fast-swimming fish.
