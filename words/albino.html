Read the full story

Albino’s story begins in Latin: albo meant “white.” White, the root describing a lack of pigmentation. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as albino. Coined by Portuguese explorers describing people with pale skin they encountered in Africa. It reached English by 1770s as albino. Adopted directly for a person or animal lacking pigmentation. Today, albino means a person or animal with a congenital absence of pigment.
