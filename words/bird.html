Read the full story

Bird’s story begins in Old English: bridd meant “young bird.” Meant a young bird or chick; the general word was 'fugol', fowl. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as brid / bird. The 'r' and 'i' swapped places, just as Old English 'thridda' became 'third'. It reached English by 1500s as bird. Replaced 'fowl' as the general word, and 'fowl' narrowed to poultry. Today, bird means a feathered, winged, egg-laying animal.
