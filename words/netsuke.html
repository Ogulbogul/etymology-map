Read the full story

Netsuke’s story begins in Japanese: netsuke meant “a small carved fastening toggle.” Named the small carved toggle used to fasten a pouch to a kimono sash. It reached English by 1800s as netsuke. Toggles used to hang pouches from the sash of a kimono; Edmund de Waal's 'The Hare with Amber Eyes' (2010) tells of a netsuke collection. Today, netsuke means a small, intricately carved Japanese toggle, now collected as miniature sculpture.
