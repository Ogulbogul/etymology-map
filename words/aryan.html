Read the full story

Aryan’s story begins in Sanskrit: arya meant “noble; compatriot.” An ethno-cultural self-designation meaning roughly 'noble, of good family'. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Old Persian as ariya. The related Iranian self-designation, source of the name 'Iran'. From there it passed into German as Arier by 1819. Philologist Friedrich Schlegel linked the word to Indo-European studies. It reached English by 1839 as Aryan. Adopted from German scholarship as a linguistic term, later distorted into a racial category. Today, aryan means historically a self-designation of the ancient Indo-Iranian peoples; in the 19th-20th centuries, misappropriated as a pseudo-scientific racial category.
