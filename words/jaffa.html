Read the full story

Jaffa’s story begins in Hebrew: Yafo meant “Jaffa (a port).” One of the world's oldest ports, already named in Egyptian records of the 1400s BC; tradition links the name to 'yafe', beautiful. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Arabic as Yāfā. Growers here developed the thick-skinned Shamouti orange in the mid-1800s, ideal for long sea voyages. It reached English by 1880s as Jaffa. Shipped to Britain in huge numbers; McVitie's launched Jaffa Cakes in 1927. Today, jaffa means a large, thick-skinned sweet orange, named after the port of Jaffa.
