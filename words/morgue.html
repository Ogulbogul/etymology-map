Read the full story

Morgue’s story begins in Vulgar Latin: murrum meant “muzzle, snout.” A word for a muzzle or snout, giving rise to a verb for making a face. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as morguer. 'to look solemnly', describing a stern facial expression. From there it passed into French as morgue by medieval. Shifted from an expression to the name of a Paris building where unidentified bodies were displayed for identification. It reached English by 1821 as morgue. Borrowed for a facility housing unclaimed dead bodies. Today, morgue means a place where dead bodies are kept before burial or identification.
