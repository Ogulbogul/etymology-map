Read the full story

Bedouin’s story begins in Arabic: badawi meant “desert dweller.” From badw, "desert"; badawin was the plural, "desert dwellers". By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as bedüin. Adopted during the Crusades, mistaking the Arabic plural ending for part of the word. It reached English by c. 1400 as bedouin. Settled into English for the nomadic tribes of the Arabian deserts. Today, bedouin means a member of a nomadic Arab people traditionally inhabiting the deserts of the Middle East and North Africa.
