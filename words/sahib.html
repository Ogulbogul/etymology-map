Read the full story

Sahib’s story begins in Arabic: sahib meant “friend, master.” From sahiba, "he accompanied"; originally meant "friend, companion," later "master". By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Hindi/Urdu as sahib. Arabic 'companion, owner'; Indians used it to address British officials. It reached English by 1670s as sahib. Used in colonial India as a respectful title for Europeans. Today, sahib means a respectful title or form of address, historically used in South Asia for a man of authority or status.
