Read the full story

Tether’s story begins in Old Norse: tjóðr meant “tether.” A tether for grazing animals. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as tethir. Borrowed in the north of England. It reached English by 1500s as tether. 'at the end of one's tether' dates from the 1700s. Today, tether means a rope or chain tying an animal to a spot.
