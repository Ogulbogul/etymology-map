Read the full story

Mosque’s story begins in Arabic: masjid meant “place of worship.” From sajada, "he worshipped," plus the place-prefix ma-. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as mezquita. Adopted during centuries of Muslim rule in Iberia. From there it passed into Italian as moschea by 1500s. Reshaped as it passed through Mediterranean trade. French picked it up next, as mosquee, around 1500s. Adapted from Italian. It reached English by 1717 as mosque. Standardized after centuries of mangled Middle English spellings like "moseak". Today, mosque means a Muslim place of worship.
