Read the full story

Calculus’s story begins in Latin: calculus meant “pebble.” A small pebble, from 'calx', limestone; Romans counted with pebbles on a board. By 1680s, the word had taken hold in Modern Latin as calculus. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz published his 'calculus differentialis', a method of calculation, in 1684. It reached English by 1660s as calculus. Doctors kept the old sense for kidney and bladder stones. Today, calculus means a branch of mathematics dealing with rates of change; a hard stone in the body.
