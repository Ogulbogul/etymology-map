Read the full story

Zulu’s story begins in Zulu: izulu meant “sky, heaven.” The root word from which the Zulu royal clan's founder took his name. Within Zulu, it grew into uZulu by c. 1709. His descendants and followers became known as amaZulu, 'the people of Zulu'. It reached English by 1824 as Zulu. From the ancestor Zulu kaMalandela, whose name means 'heaven'; Shaka built the Zulu kingdom in the 1810s. Today, zulu means the Zulu people or language of southern Africa; also used for the letter Z in the NATO phonetic alphabet and for Coordinated Universal Time.
