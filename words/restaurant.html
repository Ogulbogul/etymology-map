Read the full story

Restaurant’s story begins in Latin: restaurare meant “to rebuild, to renew.” To rebuild, to renew, from re- (again) and staurare (to erect, related to stare). By medieval, the word had taken hold in French as restaurer. Carried into French for restoring strength, especially through food. By 1765, French speakers had reshaped it into restaurant. A restorative food or broth, later a place serving such food, present participle of restaurer. It reached English by 1827 as restaurant. Adopted directly for an establishment where meals are served. Today, restaurant means a business establishment where meals are prepared and served to paying customers.
