Read the full story

Tandoori’s story begins in Akkadian: tinūru meant “oven.” A clay oven, in cuneiform texts of ancient Mesopotamia. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as tanūr. A clay oven. From there it passed into Urdu as tandūrī by medieval. Cooked in a tandoor; the Moti Mahal restaurant in Delhi made tandoori chicken famous after 1947. It reached English by 1950s as tandoori. Spread through Indian restaurants in Britain. Today, tandoori means cooked in a clay oven (tandoor).
