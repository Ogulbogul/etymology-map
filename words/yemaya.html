Read the full story

Yemaya’s story begins in Yoruba: Yemọja meant “river goddess of motherhood and creation.” Compound of iye ("mother"), ọmọ ("child"), and ẹja ("fish"): "mother whose children are like the fish". By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as Yemayá. Creolized in Cuba as enslaved Yoruba carried her worship into Santería/Lucumí practice. It reached English by 1900s as Yemaya. Spread into English through Santería and wider diaspora spiritual writing. Today, yemaya means the Yoruba-derived orisha of the sea and motherhood, venerated across Santería and other diaspora religions.
