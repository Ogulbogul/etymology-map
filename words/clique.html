Read the full story

Clique’s story begins in Old French: cliquer meant “to click, clatter, crackle.” An echoic coinage imitating a sharp clicking or clattering sound. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in French as clique. The noise-word narrowed to name a door latch and, by association, a tight little group of people. It reached English by 1711 as clique. Borrowed for an exclusive, self-interested social set. Today, clique means a small, exclusive group of people who share interests and exclude outsiders.
