Read the full story

Jute’s story begins in Sanskrit: juta meant “matted hair, fiber.” Literally "matted hair", describing the plant's fibrous strands. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Bengali as jute. The fiber crop widely cultivated in Bengal. It reached English by 1700s-1800s as jute. Dundee's mills made it 'Juteopolis', processing Bengali jute into sacking. Today, jute means a coarse plant fiber used to make rope, twine, and sacking.
