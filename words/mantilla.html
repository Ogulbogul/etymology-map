Read the full story

Mantilla’s story begins in Spanish: manta meant “cloak.” A blanket or cloak. Within Spanish, it grew into mantilla by 1600s. 'little cloak', worn over a tall comb, the 'peineta'. It reached English by 1710s as mantilla. Borrowed in travel writing on Spain. Today, mantilla means a lace scarf worn over the hair and shoulders.
