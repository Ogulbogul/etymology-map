Read the full story

Hetman’s story begins in Middle High German: houbetman meant “captain.” 'head man, captain' (modern Hauptmann), from houbet 'head'. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in Polish as hetman. Borrowed, probably via Czech hejtman, for the Crown's army commanders; Hetman Stanisław Żółkiewski won the Battle of Klushino in 1610 and occupied Moscow. From there it passed into Ukrainian as hetman by 1648. The elected Cossack leader; Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky's 1648 uprising founded the Cossack Hetmanate. It reached English by 1700s as hetman. Used for Polish and Cossack commanders; Pavlo Skoropadsky revived the title as Hetman of Ukraine in 1918. Today, hetman means a Cossack leader or Polish military commander.
