Read the full story

Acquittal’s story begins in Medieval Latin: acquietare meant “to set free, to satisfy a claim.” To satisfy a debt, to set free, from ad- (to) and quietus (at rest). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as aquiter. Carried into French legal vocabulary for discharging an obligation or clearing a charge. It reached English by 1400s as acquittal. Settled as the English word for a formal clearing of a criminal charge. Today, acquittal means a formal declaration that a person is not guilty of a charged offense.
