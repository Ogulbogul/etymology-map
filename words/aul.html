Read the full story

Aul’s story begins in Kipchak Turkic: awıl meant “nomad camp.” A camp of yurts belonging to related families. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Russian as aul. Used for the mountain villages of the Caucasus in the tales of Pushkin, Lermontov and Tolstoy. It reached English by 1830s as aul. Kept in English translations of Russian Caucasian stories such as Tolstoy's 'Hadji Murat'. Today, aul means a village or nomad camp in the Caucasus or Central Asia.
