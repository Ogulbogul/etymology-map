Read the full story

Macaroni’s story begins in Italian: maccare meant “to bruise, crush.” A verb of uncertain deeper origin meaning to crush or batter. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Southern Italian as maccarone. Named a pasty food made of flour, cheese, and butter. From there it passed into English as macaroni by 1590s. Borrowed for the pasta dish, later shortened for it as well. It reached English by 1769 as macaroni. Extended to a fop obsessed with fashionable Continental style, via London's Macaroni Club. Today, macaroni means a tube-shaped pasta, or, archaically, a fashionable dandy.
