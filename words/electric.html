Read the full story

Electric’s story begins in Greek: elektron meant “amber.” Amber, which produces static charge when rubbed. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Latin as electricus. Coined by scientist William Gilbert from the Greek word for amber. It reached English by 1600s as electric. Settled as the word for the new phenomenon of electricity. Today, electric means relating to electricity, or full of excitement and energy.
