Read the full story

Enchant’s story begins in Latin: canere meant “to sing.” To sing, combined with in- (upon). Within Latin, it grew into incantare by ancient. To sing a magic spell over, to bewitch, from in- and cantare. From there it passed into Old French as enchanter by medieval. Carried into French for casting a magic spell, or delighting someone greatly. It reached English by 1300s as enchant. Settled as the English word for delighting someone completely, as if by magic. Today, enchant means to fill someone with great delight, or to put a magic spell on them.
