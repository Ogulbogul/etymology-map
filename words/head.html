Read the full story

Head’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: haubudą meant “head.” Often linked to Latin 'caput', as in 'capital'. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as hēafod. Lost its middle consonant to become 'hed' by the 1200s. It reached English by 1500s as head. The 'ea' spelling keeps a trace of the older two-syllable form. Today, head means the upper part of the body containing the brain.
