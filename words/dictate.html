Read the full story

Dictate’s story begins in Latin: dictare meant “to say repeatedly, to prescribe.” To say repeatedly, to prescribe, from dicere (to say). It reached English by 1620s as dictate. Settled as the English word for speaking words for another to write, or issuing orders. Today, dictate means to state words for another to write down, or to lay down rules with authority.
