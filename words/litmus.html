Read the full story

Litmus’s story begins in Old Norse: litmosi meant “dye moss.” 'dye-moss', from 'litr', colour, and 'mosi', moss: lichens used for dyeing. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in Middle Dutch as lakmoes. Dutch dyers processed the lichen into a blue dye. It reached English by 1500s as litmus. Chemists used litmus paper to test acids; 'litmus test' as a decisive test dates from the 1950s. Today, litmus means a dye from lichens that turns red in acid and blue in alkali.
