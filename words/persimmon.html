Read the full story

Persimmon’s story begins in Powhatan: pasiminan meant “a dried fruit.” Literally "a dried fruit". It reached English by 1600s as persimmon. Powhatan 'pessemmins'; John Smith said unripe ones 'draw a man's mouth awrie'. Today, persimmon means a sweet, orange fruit that must ripen fully before eating.
