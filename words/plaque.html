Read the full story

Plaque’s story begins in Middle Dutch: placke meant “a flat piece, a patch.” A flat piece, a patch, describing a flat decorative or informational panel. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as plaque. Carried into French for a flat commemorative panel. It reached English by 1860s as plaque. Settled as the English word for a commemorative flat panel, later dental buildup. Today, plaque means a flat commemorative panel, or a sticky film that builds up on teeth.
