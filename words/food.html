Read the full story

Food’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: fōdô meant “nourishment.” From the same root as 'feed' and 'fodder', and ultimately Latin 'pastor', one who feeds the flock. By 1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as fōda. Rare in Old English, where 'mete' (meat) was the usual word for food. It reached English by 1300s as food. Became the general word as 'meat' narrowed to mean animal flesh. Today, food means anything eaten to provide nourishment.
