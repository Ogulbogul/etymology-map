Read the full story

Lord’s story begins in Old English: hlāford meant “master.” From 'hlāfweard', loaf-guardian: the head of the household who provided its bread. By 1100s-1400s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as lord. Shortened from 'loverd' by the 1300s. It reached English by 1500s as lord. Used for God as a translation of Latin 'Dominus'. Today, lord means a man of noble rank or high authority.
