Read the full story

Intaglio’s story begins in Late Latin: taliare meant “to split, cut.” A verb meaning to split or cut. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as tagliare. Kept the sense of cutting. By medieval, Italian speakers had reshaped it into intagliare. In- 'into' plus tagliare 'to cut' - to engrave by cutting into a surface. It reached English by 1640s as intaglio. Borrowed as a noun for an image incised into a hard surface. Today, intaglio means a design incised or engraved into a hard surface.
