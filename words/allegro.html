Read the full story

Allegro’s story begins in Italian: allegro meant “cheerful, lively.” Literally cheerful, lively, adopted as a musical tempo marking. It reached English by 1700s as allegro. Italian for 'cheerful, lively'; the tempo sense came from Italian sonata movements. Today, allegro means a fast, lively musical tempo, or a piece played at that tempo.
