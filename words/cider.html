Read the full story

Cider’s story begins in Hebrew: shekar meant “strong drink.” A Hebrew word for any intoxicating drink, borrowed through Greek 'sikera'. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Late Latin as sikera. Used in the Latin Bible for strong drink. From there it passed into Old French as sidre by medieval. Normandy became famous for apple cider. It reached English by 1300s as cider. In the US 'cider' can mean unfiltered apple juice. Today, cider means an alcoholic drink made from apples.
