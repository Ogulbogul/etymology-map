Read the full story

Eat’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: etaną meant “to eat.” Cognate with Latin 'edere', as in 'edible'. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as etan. An animal's eating was 'fretan', to devour, which became 'fret'. It reached English by 1500s as eat. Its past tense 'ate' is still pronounced 'et' in much of Britain. Today, eat means to put food into the mouth and swallow it.
