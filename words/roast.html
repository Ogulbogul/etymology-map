Read the full story

Roast’s story begins in Frankish: raustjan meant “to grill.” A Germanic verb for cooking on a grid, related to German 'Rost', grill. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as rostir. Used for meat turned on a spit before an open fire. It reached English by 1200s as roast. The sense 'to mock harshly' dates from the 1700s; comedic 'roasts' followed. Today, roast means to cook food in an oven or over a fire; to mock someone playfully.
