Read the full story

Inertia’s story begins in Latin: iners meant “idle, unskilled.” Literally unskilled, idle, from in- (without) and ars (skill). It reached English by 1710s as inertia. Adopted by physicists for a body's resistance to a change in motion. Today, inertia means the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of motion.
