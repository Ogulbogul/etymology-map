Read the full story

Gospel’s story begins in Greek: euangélion meant “good news.” 'good news', eu 'good' + angelia 'message'; Mark's Gospel opens 'The beginning of the euangelion of Jesus Christ'. By 900s, the word had taken hold in Old English as gōdspel. A word-for-word translation, gōd 'good' + spel 'news, story'; Aldred wrote an Old English gloss into the Lindisfarne Gospels in the 900s. It reached English by 1200s as gospel. The first part was soon heard as 'God', 'God-story'; Thomas A. Dorsey, 'the father of gospel music', shaped the Black church music style from the 1930s. Today, gospel means the teaching of Christ; one of the first four books of the New Testament; a music style.
