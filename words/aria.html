Read the full story

Aria’s story begins in Greek: aer meant “mist, haze; air.” Mist, haze, clouds, later broadened to mean the atmosphere or air. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as aer. Romans used it for the lower, breathable air, as opposed to 'aether' above. From there it passed into Italian as aria by 1600s. Literally air, named an expressive solo vocal piece in an opera. It reached English by 1700s as aria. Handel's Italian operas in London made the word familiar to English audiences. Today, aria means a self-contained solo vocal piece within an opera or oratorio.
