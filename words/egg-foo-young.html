Read the full story

Egg foo young’s story begins in Cantonese: fu4 jung4 daan2 meant “an egg omelet dish.” Literally 'hibiscus egg', naming an omelet dish named for its flower-like appearance. It reached English by late 1800s-early 1900s as egg foo young. Adapted with reversed word order into Chinese-American restaurant menus. Today, egg foo young means a Chinese-American omelet dish made with vegetables and meat, typically served with gravy.
