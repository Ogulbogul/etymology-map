Read the full story

Stilt’s story begins in Scandinavian: stylta meant “stilt, prop.” Probably from a Norse or Low German source; Norwegian 'stylta' means a stilt. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as stilte. A crutch, or the handle of a plough. It reached English by 1500s as stilt. The long-legged wading bird called a stilt is named after it. Today, stilt means either of a pair of poles for walking raised above the ground.
