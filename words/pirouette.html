Read the full story

Pirouette’s story begins in Gallo-Roman: pir- meant “a peg, plug.” A root meaning peg or plug. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as piruolo. Named a spinning peg top, a child's toy. From there it passed into French as pirouette by 1400s. Borrowed and given a diminutive ending, literally 'little peg', for the spinning toy and later a dancer's spin. It reached English by 1706 as pirouette. Adopted for a rapid balletic turn on one leg. Today, pirouette means a rapid spin on one foot, especially in ballet.
