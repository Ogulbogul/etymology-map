Read the full story

Lance’s story begins in Latin: lancea meant “light spear.” Roman writers said the word came from Spain or Gaul. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as lance. The long spear of the mounted knight, couched under the arm to charge. It reached English by 1300s as lance. 'freelance' was coined by Walter Scott in 1819 for a mercenary knight. Today, lance means a long spear used by mounted knights; to cut open with a blade.
