Read the full story

Static’s story begins in Greek: histanai meant “to stand.” To stand, to cause to stand. Within Greek, it grew into statikos by ancient. Causing to stand still, weighing, from histanai. From there it passed into New Latin as staticus by 1600s. Carried into scholarly Latin for the science of bodies at rest. It reached English by 1600s as static. Settled as the English word for something not moving or changing. Today, static means not moving or changing, or interference in an electronic signal.
