Read the full story

Massacre’s story begins in Vulgar Latin: macellum meant “meat market.” Possibly from 'macellarius', butcher. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as macecre. A butchery or shambles. It reached English by 1580s as massacre. The St Bartholomew's Day Massacre of 1572 spread it. Today, massacre means the brutal killing of many people.
