Read the full story

Waratah’s story begins in Dharug: warada meant “waratah shrub.” The red-flowered shrub of the Sydney sandstone country; the botanist James Edward Smith described it in 1793 from specimens sent from the new colony. It reached English by 1790s as waratah. Adopted by the colonists; New South Wales proclaimed it the state's floral emblem in 1962. Today, waratah means an Australian shrub with large crimson flower heads, the floral emblem of New South Wales.
