Read the full story

Decadent’s story begins in Latin: cadere meant “to fall.” To fall, combined with de- (down). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Late Latin as decadere. To fall down, to decline, from de- and cadere. From there it passed into French as decadent by 1800s. Carried into French for a state of moral or cultural decline, later self-indulgent luxury. It reached English by 1830s as decadent. Adopted from French for something excessively self-indulgent or in decline. Today, decadent means excessively self-indulgent, especially in luxury and pleasure.
