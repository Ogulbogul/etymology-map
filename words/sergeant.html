Read the full story

Sergeant’s story begins in Latin: servientem meant “serving.” From 'servire', to serve, the same root as 'servant'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as sergent. A servant or retainer, later an officer who enforced a lord's orders. It reached English by 1500s as sergeant. Fixed as a military rank in the 16th century. Today, sergeant means a non-commissioned military rank; a police rank above constable.
