Read the full story

Torso’s story begins in Greek: thyrsos meant “a stalk, staff.” A ceremonial staff, also used to mean a plant stalk. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as thyrsus. Adopted through Greek influence on Roman art and ritual. From there it passed into Italian as torso by medieval. Came to mean a plant stump, then a statue's trunk without limbs or head. It reached English by 1700s as torso. Adopted directly from Italian. Today, torso means the trunk of the human body, excluding the head and limbs.
