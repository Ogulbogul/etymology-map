Read the full story

Acid’s story begins in Latin: acidus meant “sour.” From 'acere', to be sour, the root of 'acerbic'. It reached English by 1620s as acid. The chemical sense dates from the 1600s; 'acid' as LSD from the 1960s; first recorded in 1626 in Francis Bacon's natural-history work 'Sylva Sylvarum'. Today, acid means a sour, corrosive chemical substance.
