Read the full story

Magic’s story begins in Old Persian: magush meant “member of the priestly class.” Named a member of the learned, priestly Magi caste. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Greek as magikos / magike. Greek writers turned the Magi's reputed powers into an adjective for sorcery. From there it passed into Late Latin as magice by ancient. Carried the sense of sorcery into Latin. Old French picked it up next, as magique, around 1300s. Passed into French as both noun and adjective. It reached English by late 1300s as magic. Settled into English describing hidden supernatural forces, later also stage illusion. Today, magic means the power of apparently influencing events using supernatural forces, or the practice of conjuring tricks for entertainment.
