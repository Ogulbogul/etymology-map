Read the full story

Compose’s story begins in Latin: com ponere meant “to put together.” To put together, from com- (together) and ponere (to place). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as composer. Carried into French for arranging parts into a whole. It reached English by 1400s as compose. Settled as the English word for creating something by putting parts together. Today, compose means to create a piece of music or writing, or to make up the parts of something.
