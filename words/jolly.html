Read the full story

Jolly’s story begins in Old Norse: jol meant “yule feast.” Possibly from Old Norse 'jól', the midwinter feast, or Latin 'gaudium'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as jolif. Described youthful gaiety. It reached English by 1300s as jolly. 'jolly' as 'very' in British slang dates from the 1500s. Today, jolly means happy and cheerful.
