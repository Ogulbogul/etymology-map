Read the full story

Doctor’s story begins in Latin: docere meant “to teach.” To teach. Within Latin, it grew into doctor by ancient. A teacher, one who teaches, from docere. From there it passed into Old French as doctour by medieval. Carried into French for a highly learned person, especially in medicine. It reached English by 1300s as doctor. Settled as the English word for a person qualified to practice medicine. Today, doctor means a person qualified to practice medicine, or one holding the highest academic degree.
