Read the full story

Porcelain’s story begins in Italian: porcellana meant “a cowrie shell, little pig.” Literally little pig, originally the name of a cowrie shell whose curved sheen resembled a pig's back. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in French as porcelaine. Transferred from the glossy shell to the glossy fine ceramic imported from China. It reached English by 1590s as porcelain. Settled as the English word for fine translucent ceramic ware. Today, porcelain means a hard, translucent ceramic material, also called china.
