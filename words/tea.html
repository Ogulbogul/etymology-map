Read the full story

Tea’s story begins in Min Chinese (Amoy): te meant “tea.” The pronunciation used in the Fujian coastal dialect of Chinese traders. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Malay as teh. Picked up by Dutch traders at Southeast Asian ports. From there it passed into Dutch as thee by 1600s. Spread across Europe through Dutch East India Company trade. It reached English by 1650s as tea. 'te' came by sea through Dutch traders from Amoy; overland routes spread 'cha' to Russia, Persia and India. Today, tea means a hot drink made by infusing dried tea leaves in water.
