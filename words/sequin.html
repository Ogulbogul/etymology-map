Read the full story

Sequin’s story begins in Arabic: sikka meant “a coin die.” Meaning "a minting die", the tool used to stamp coins. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as zecchino. The name of a Venetian gold coin. From there it passed into French as sequin by 1600s. Later applied to small, shiny coin-like ornaments. It reached English by 1600s-1700s as sequin. Adopted from French for decorative discs on clothing. Today, sequin means a small, shiny disc sewn onto clothing for decoration.
