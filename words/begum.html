Read the full story

Begum’s story begins in East Turkic: beg + -um meant “lady, princess.” Beg meant "lord, chief"; -um was a feminine suffix of nobility. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as begum. Feminine of the Turkic title 'beg', lord; Mughal royal women held the title. From there it passed into Urdu as begam by 1600s. Used as a title for women of high rank under the Mughal Empire. It reached English by 1630s as begum. Entered English through British contact with Mughal and later South Asian courts. Today, begum means a Muslim woman of high rank, or a title of respect for a married Muslim woman, especially in South Asia.
