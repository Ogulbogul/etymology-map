Read the full story

Garden’s story begins in Frankish: gardo meant “enclosure.” A Germanic word for an enclosure, related to English 'yard'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old North French as gardin. Norman form; Parisian French said 'jardin'. It reached English by 1300s as garden. So native English 'yard' and French-derived 'garden' share one Germanic root. Today, garden means a piece of ground used for growing flowers, fruit or vegetables.
