Read the full story

A cappella’s story begins in Italian: a cappella meant “in the chapel style.” Literally in the chapel style, referring to unaccompanied choral singing. It reached English by 1870s as a cappella. Italian for 'in the chapel style', recalling unaccompanied church choirs; the phrase spread with Victorian choral societies. Today, a cappella means vocal music performed without instrumental accompaniment.
