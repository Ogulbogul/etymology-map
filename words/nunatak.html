Read the full story

Nunatak’s story begins in Kalaallisut (Greenlandic): nunataq meant “a rocky peak surrounded by ice.” Combined 'nuna' (land) with a suffix meaning 'thing pertaining to'. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Danish as nunatak. Adopted through Danish scientific and colonial administration of Greenland. It reached English by 1870s as nunatak. Borrowed into English geological and glaciological terminology. Today, nunatak means an isolated peak or ridge of rock that protrudes above the surface of a glacier or ice sheet.
