Read the full story

Chit’s story begins in Sanskrit: citra meant “something marked or distinct.” Root meaning 'distinctly marked', underlying the later word for a marked note. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Marathi as chitthi. Came to mean a written letter or note. From there it passed into Hindi as chitty by 1690s. Anglo-Indian traders adopted the word for a signed note or pass. It reached English by 1776 as chit. Shortened from 'chitty' in everyday British-Indian use. Today, chit means a short note, memorandum, or signed voucher, especially for a small debt or authorization.
