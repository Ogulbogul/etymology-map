Read the full story

Wallah’s story begins in Sanskrit: pala meant “protector, guardian.” Meant 'protector', from a root meaning 'to protect'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Hindi as -wala. Became a common suffix meaning 'the person connected with X'. It reached English by 1776 as wallah. British colonials mistook the suffix for a standalone noun meaning 'fellow, man'. Today, wallah means informal British English for a person associated with a particular job or activity (e.g., 'the tea wallah').
