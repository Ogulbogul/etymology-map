Read the full story

Favela’s story begins in Portuguese: favela meant “a thorny backlands shrub.” A thorny shrub (Cnidoscolus quercifolius) covering the Morro da Favela hill at Canudos, in the Bahian backlands, where the army besieged Antônio Conselheiro's settlement in 1896-97. By 1897, the word had taken hold in Brazilian Portuguese as favela. Soldiers back from Canudos, promised housing that never came, settled a hill in Rio de Janeiro and called it Morro da Favela; the name spread to every such settlement. It reached English by 1900s as favela. Used for Brazilian shantytowns; the 2002 film City of God, set in Rio's Cidade de Deus, made the word widely known abroad. Today, favela means an informal settlement or shantytown in Brazil.
