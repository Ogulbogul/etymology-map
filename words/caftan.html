Read the full story

Caftan’s story begins in Persian: khaftan meant “long tunic.” A long tunic-like garment tied at the waist. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as qaftan. Spread the garment and its name through the Islamic world. From there it passed into Turkish as qaftan by 1500s. Became a symbol of Ottoman court dress. It reached English by 1590s as caftan / kaftan. Entered English describing Ottoman and Egyptian dress; later became a Western fashion term. Today, caftan means a long tunic-like garment, often tied with a sash, or a similar loose dress worn as fashionable clothing.
