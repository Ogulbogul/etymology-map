Read the full story

Uproar’s story begins in Dutch: oproer meant “a riot, commotion.” Combining "op" (up) and "roer" (stir, motion), meaning a riot or commotion. It reached English by 1500s as uproar. Adopted and reshaped through folk association with "roar". Today, uproar means a loud and excited disturbance, or a public outcry.
