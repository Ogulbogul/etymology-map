Read the full story

Traitor’s story begins in Latin: tradere meant “to hand over, to betray.” To hand over, to betray, from trans- (across) and dare (to give). Within Latin, it grew into traditor by ancient. One who hands over or betrays, from tradere. From there it passed into Old French as traitre by medieval. Carried into French for a person who betrays trust. It reached English by 1200s as traitor. Settled as the English word for a person who betrays their country or cause. Today, traitor means a person who betrays their country, a cause, or someone's trust.
