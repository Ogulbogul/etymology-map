Read the full story

Viscount’s story begins in Medieval Latin: vice comes meant “a deputy count.” Literally deputy count, from vice- (in place of) and comes (count). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as viscounte. Settled in French for a nobleman ranking just below a count. It reached English by 1400s as viscount. Latin 'vice comes', deputy count; the first English viscount was created in 1440. Today, viscount means a nobleman ranking below an earl or count and above a baron.
