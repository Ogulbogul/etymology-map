Read the full story

Colonel’s story begins in Latin: columna meant “a pillar, column.” A plain word for a pillar. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as colonnella. Named the commander at the head of a regiment's 'little column' of soldiers. From there it passed into French as coronel by 1500s. Borrowed from Italian with a sound change (dissimilation) that altered the first consonant. It reached English by 1580s as colonel. The spelling was reformed to match the Italian original, though the French-derived pronunciation persisted. Today, colonel means a senior military officer rank, above lieutenant colonel and below brigadier general.
