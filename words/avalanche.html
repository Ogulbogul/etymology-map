Read the full story

Avalanche’s story begins in Franco-Provencal: lavanche meant “a sliding mass of snow.” A mass of snow and ice sliding rapidly down a mountainside. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in French as avalanche. Reshaped in French for the sudden mountain snowslide. It reached English by 1760s as avalanche. Popularized by Alpine travel writers; the Swiss French form was influenced by 'avaler', to descend. Today, avalanche means a mass of snow, ice, or rock falling rapidly down a mountainside.
