Read the full story

Prince’s story begins in Latin: princeps meant “first citizen, leader.” From 'primus', first, and 'capere', to take; Augustus used it as a modest-sounding title. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as prince. Applied to any sovereign ruler, not just a king's son. It reached English by 1200s as prince. Gradually narrowed to the son of a monarch. Today, prince means the son of a king or queen; a ruler of a principality.
