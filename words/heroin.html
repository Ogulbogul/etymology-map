Read the full story

Heroin’s story begins in Greek: hḗrōs meant “hero.” 'hero, demigod', as in the heroes of Homer's Iliad. By 1898, the word had taken hold in German as Heroin. Bayer trademarked it in 1898 and sold it as a cough medicine, supposedly because it made users feel heroic. It reached English by 1898 as heroin. Its addictiveness soon became clear, and the US banned its manufacture in 1924. Today, heroin means a highly addictive narcotic drug made from morphine.
