Read the full story

Nickname’s story begins in Old English: ēaca meant “addition.” 'an addition, increase', related to the verb ēcan 'to add', which survives in 'to eke out'. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as ekename. 'an additional name'; the Promptorium Parvulorum, the first English-Latin dictionary (c. 1440), lists both 'neke name, or eke name', as 'an ekename' was being heard as 'a nekename'. It reached English by 1500s as nickname. The n stolen from the article stuck, and the vowel shifted to 'nick'. Today, nickname means a familiar or humorous name used instead of a real name.
