Read the full story

Cheap’s story begins in Latin: caupo meant “innkeeper, huckster.” A tavern-keeper or petty trader, the kind of Roman merchant Germanic peoples met along the frontier. By ancient, the word had taken hold in West Germanic as *kaupōn. Turned into a verb 'to trade', the source of German kaufen 'to buy'. From there it passed into Old English as cēap by medieval. A market or bargain, surviving in London's Cheapside and towns like Chipping Norton. It reached English by 1500s as cheap. Shortened from 'good cheap', a good bargain, to mean low-priced. Today, cheap means low in price; of poor quality.
