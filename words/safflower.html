Read the full story

Safflower’s story begins in Arabic: asfar meant “yellow.” Meant simply "yellow," describing the plant's dye. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as asfiore. Adapted the Arabic color word into a plant name. From there it passed into Middle French as safleur by 1500s. Folk etymology reshaped it to resemble "safran" (saffron) and "fleur" (flower). Dutch picked it up next, as saffloer, around 1500s. Passed through Dutch trade networks. It reached English by 1500s as safflower. Settled into its modern English form. Today, safflower means a thistle-like plant grown for its orange-red flowers, used to make dye, cooking oil, and food coloring.
