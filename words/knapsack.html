Read the full story

Knapsack’s story begins in German: Knapsack meant “a soldier's food bag.” Low German compound of 'knappen' (to snap, to eat) and 'Sack' (bag) — literally an 'eating bag'. By 1500s-1600s, the word had taken hold in Dutch as knapzak. Parallel form current alongside the Low German word as it spread through trading and military contacts. It reached English by c. 1600 as knapsack. Adopted directly for a soldier's or traveler's pack. Today, knapsack means a bag carried on the back, especially for hiking or travel.
