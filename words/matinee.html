Read the full story

Matinee’s story begins in Latin: matutinus meant “of the morning.” By medieval, the word had taken hold in French as matinée. Came to mean the morning itself, then a daytime performance. It reached English by 1800s as matinee. Adopted for an afternoon theatrical or film performance. Today, matinee means an afternoon performance of a play, film, or other entertainment.
