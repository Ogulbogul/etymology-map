Read the full story

Isicathamiya’s story begins in Zulu: -cathama meant “to walk softly, tread carefully.” The verb root describing stealthy, careful movement. By 1920s, the word had taken hold in Zulu as isicathamiya. Named for the gentle, tiptoeing dance steps of migrant mineworkers who sang quietly at night hostels. It reached English by 1980s as isicathamiya. Entered English through the international popularity of groups like Ladysmith Black Mambazo. Today, isicathamiya means a style of soft, harmonized Zulu a cappella singing, made internationally famous by groups such as Ladysmith Black Mambazo.
