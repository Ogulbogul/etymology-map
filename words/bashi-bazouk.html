Read the full story

Bashi-bazouk’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: başıbozuk meant “irregular soldier.” Literally 'head out of order', i.e. leaderless: irregular troops without uniform or regular pay. It reached English by 1850s as bashi-bazouk. British officers met them in the Crimean War; their 1876 massacres in Bulgaria became notorious through Gladstone's pamphlet 'Bulgarian Horrors'. Today, bashi-bazouk means an irregular, undisciplined soldier of the Ottoman army.
