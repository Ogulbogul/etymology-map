Read the full story

Relieve’s story begins in Latin: levis meant “light, not heavy.” Light, not heavy, combined with re- (again). Within Latin, it grew into relevare by ancient. To lift up again, to lighten, from re- and levis. From there it passed into Old French as relever by medieval. Carried into French for lightening a burden or raising something up. It reached English by 1300s as relieve. Settled as the English word for lessening pain, distress, or a burden. Today, relieve means to lessen pain, distress, or a burden, bringing comfort.
