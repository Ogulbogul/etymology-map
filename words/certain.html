Read the full story

Certain’s story begins in Latin: certus meant “determined, settled.” Determined, settled, from cernere (to sift, to determine). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as certain. Carried into French for something known for sure. It reached English by 1300s as certain. Settled as the English word for something known without doubt. Today, certain means known for sure, without doubt.
