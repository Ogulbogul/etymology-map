Read the full story

Genet’s story begins in Arabic: jarnait meant “small civet-like animal.” Named the small civet-like carnivore of North Africa. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as gineta. Adopted the animal's name during centuries of contact across the Strait of Gibraltar. From there it passed into Old French as genete by 1200s. Carried into French natural history vocabulary. It reached English by late 1400s as genet. Entered English describing the slender, spotted nocturnal carnivore. Today, genet means a small, slender, nocturnal carnivore related to the civet, found in Africa and parts of Europe.
