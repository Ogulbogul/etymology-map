Read the full story

Lascar’s story begins in Persian: lashkar meant “army, camp.” Meant "army" or "camp". By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Hindi as lashkari. Narrowed to mean a soldier, and later a sailor. From there it passed into Portuguese as lachar by 1600s. Portuguese colonial sailors used it for South Asian crew members. It reached English by 1620s as lascar. Persian 'lashkar', army; lascars made up much of the crew on British merchant ships. Today, lascar means historically, a sailor from India or Southeast Asia employed on European merchant ships.
