Read the full story

Spiral’s story begins in Greek: speira meant “a coil, a twist.” A coil, a twist, anything wound around. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as spira. Archimedes wrote a treatise 'On Spirals'. From there it passed into Middle French as spiral by 1500s. From Medieval Latin spiralis. It reached English by 1550s as spiral. Settled as the English word for a curve winding continuously around a center. Today, spiral means a curve that winds continuously around a fixed central point, moving outward or inward.
