Read the full story

Dish’s story begins in Greek: dískos meant “discus, quoit.” A thrown disc; Myron's bronze Discobolus (c. 450 BC) shows an athlete about to throw one. By classical, the word had taken hold in Latin as discus. A quoit, then a round platter; the same word later gave English desk, dais, disc and disk. It reached English by 1300s as dish. Borrowed into Old English as disc 'plate'; the 'satellite dish' followed the first satellite TV links of the 1960s. Today, dish means a shallow container for cooking or serving food; a particular food.
