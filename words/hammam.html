Read the full story

Hammam’s story begins in Arabic: ḥammām meant “bath house.” From 'ḥamma', to heat; the Islamic world kept up the Roman public bath. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Ottoman Turkish as hamam. The architect Sinan built great domed baths in Istanbul, such as the Haseki Hürrem hamam of 1556. It reached English by 1600s as hammam. The diplomat David Urquhart promoted 'Turkish baths' in Britain; the first opened near Blarney in Ireland in 1856. Today, hammam means a Turkish or Middle Eastern steam bath.
