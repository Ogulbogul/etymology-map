Read the full story

Parachute’s story begins in Italian: parare meant “to shield.” An Italian verb meaning to ward off, as in 'parasol'. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in French as parachute. 'para-', guard against, plus 'chute', fall; coined by Jean-Pierre Blanchard around 1785. It reached English by 1785 as parachute. First used for drops from balloons; military paratroopers date from the 1920s. Today, parachute means a device for slowing a fall.
