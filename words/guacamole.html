Read the full story

Guacamole’s story begins in Nahuatl: ahuacamolli meant “avocado sauce.” Combining "ahuacatl" (avocado) and "molli" (sauce). By 1500s-1600s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as guacamole. Adopted from Nahuatl via Spanish colonial contact in Mexico. It reached English by 1900s as guacamole. Adopted as Mexican cuisine spread internationally. Today, guacamole means a dip or side dish made from mashed avocado, typically with lime and seasonings.
