Read the full story

Bulgur’s story begins in Persian: barghul meant “cracked wheat.” Named parboiled, cracked wheat, meaning roughly "roughly crushed". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as burghul. Spread the grain and its name across the medieval Middle East. From there it passed into Ottoman Turkish as bulgur by 1800s. The sounds were rearranged in Turkish pronunciation. It reached English by 1926 as bulgur. Entered English food writing describing the ancient Middle Eastern grain staple. Today, bulgur means a cereal food made from parboiled, dried, and cracked wheat.
