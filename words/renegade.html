Read the full story

Renegade’s story begins in Spanish: renegado meant “one who has renounced their faith.” One who has renounced their faith or allegiance, from renegar (to deny). It reached English by 1580s as renegade. Settled as the word for someone who deserts a cause or group. Today, renegade means a person who abandons their group or cause for another, often seen as rebellious.
