Read the full story

Araba’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: araba meant “cart, carriage.” Ox-drawn, often painted and gilded arabas carried Ottoman women on outings. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Russian as arba. Used for the high-wheeled carts of Crimea and the Caucasus. It reached English by 1810s as araba. Described by English travellers in the Ottoman Empire and Crimea. Today, araba means a heavy covered cart or carriage of Turkey and the Caucasus.
