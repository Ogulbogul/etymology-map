Read the full story

Mammoth’s story begins in Siberian (Yakut): mangont meant “creature believed to burrow underground.” A folk term for the tusked remains found frozen in the permafrost. By 1600s-1700s, the word had taken hold in Russian as mamont. Adopted from Siberian accounts of the giant tusks and bones. It reached English by 1700s as mammoth. Later also used to describe anything of huge size. Today, mammoth means an extinct, elephant-like mammal with long tusks and thick fur; informally, anything huge.
