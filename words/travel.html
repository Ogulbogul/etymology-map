Read the full story

Travel’s story begins in Late Latin: trepalium meant “torture instrument.” A three-staked torture device, from 'tres', three, and 'palus', stake. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as travailler. To torment, then to toil; modern French 'travailler' means to work. It reached English by 1300s as travel. A variant of 'travail'; medieval journeys were so arduous that toil came to mean journeying. Today, travel means to go from one place to another, especially over a distance.
