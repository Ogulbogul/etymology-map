Read the full story

Masochism’s story begins in German: Sacher-Masoch meant “a family name.” Leopold von Sacher-Masoch, born in Lemberg (Lviv) in 1836, wrote the novel Venus in Furs (1870). Within German, it grew into Masochismus by 1890. Richard von Krafft-Ebing coined it in 1890, while Sacher-Masoch was still alive and much to his displeasure. It reached English by 1893 as masochism. Entered English through the 1893 translation of Krafft-Ebing's Psychopathia Sexualis. Today, masochism means pleasure, especially sexual, from one's own pain or humiliation.
