Read the full story

Abalone’s story begins in Rumsen (Ohlone): aulun meant “red abalone.” Named the red abalone shellfish common in Monterey Bay. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as abulón. Spanish missionaries recorded it from the Rumsen people around Monterey Bay. It reached English by 1850s as abalone. Anglicized during the California Gold Rush era as the shellfish trade grew. Today, abalone means a large edible sea snail prized for its meat and iridescent mother-of-pearl shell.
