Read the full story

Routine’s story begins in Latin: rumpere meant “to break.” The verb 'to break', applied to a road broken or cut through a forest. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in French as route. From rupta (via), literally '(a road) broken open'. By 1500s, French speakers had reshaped it into routine. Route plus the suffix -ine, coined for a habitual, well-worn course of action. It reached English by 1670s as routine. Adopted for any fixed, habitual way of doing things. Today, routine means a fixed, regular way of doing things.
