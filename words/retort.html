Read the full story

Retort’s story begins in Latin: torquere meant “to twist.” To twist, combined with re- (back). Within Latin, it grew into retorquere by ancient. To twist back, to turn against, from re- and torquere. It reached English by 1500s as retort. Settled as the English word for a quick, sharp, or witty reply. Today, retort means a quick, sharp, or witty reply, especially to a criticism.
