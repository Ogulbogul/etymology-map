Read the full story

Terracotta’s story begins in Latin: terra + cocta meant “baked earth.” 'earth' and the past participle of coquere 'to cook' combined. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as terra cotta. Kept the compound sense for fired, unglazed clay. It reached English by 1722 as terracotta. Borrowed for the hard, brownish-red earthenware and its color. Today, terracotta means a hard, unglazed, brownish-red earthenware, or its color.
