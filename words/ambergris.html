Read the full story

Ambergris’s story begins in Arabic: 'anbar meant “ambergris.” Named the waxy substance found floating in tropical seas. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Medieval Latin as ambre. Carried into Europe via Crusade-era trade. From there it passed into Old French as ambre gris by 1200s. "gris" (gray) was added to distinguish it from amber resin. It reached English by early 1400s as ambergris. Its true origin as a whale secretion remained mysterious in Europe for centuries. Today, ambergris means a waxy substance from the intestines of sperm whales, historically prized as a fixative in perfumes.
