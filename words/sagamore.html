Read the full story

Sagamore’s story begins in Eastern Abenaki: sakəma meant “a chief or leader.” Named a lesser chief elected to lead a single band, related to Proto-Algonquian 'sakima·wa'. It reached English by 1613 as sagamore. Samoset, who greeted the Plymouth Pilgrims in 1621, was an Abenaki sagamore. Today, sagamore means historically, a chief or leader among Algonquian peoples of the northeastern United States; later used as an honorary or jocular title.
