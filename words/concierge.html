Read the full story

Concierge’s story begins in Latin: servus meant “a slave, servant.” The word for a slave or servant. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Vulgar Latin as conservius. Com- 'together' plus servus, for a fellow servant. From there it passed into French as concierge by 1100s. Softened from lowly servitude to a trusted caretaker role, eventually a high royal office. It reached English by 1640s as concierge. Borrowed for the caretaker of a building who assists its occupants. Today, concierge means a hotel or apartment building employee who assists guests or residents.
