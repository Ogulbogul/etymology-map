Read the full story

Wind’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: windaz meant “wind.” Cognate with Latin 'ventus', as in 'vent' and 'ventilate'. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as wind. Rhymed with 'find' until the 1700s, when its vowel shortened. It reached English by 1500s as wind. 'window' comes from Old Norse 'vindauga', wind-eye. Today, wind means air moving naturally across the Earth.
