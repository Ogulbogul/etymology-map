Read the full story

Bulwark’s story begins in Middle Dutch: bolwerc meant “log rampart.” 'bole', tree trunk, plus 'werc', work: a rampart of logs. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as bulwerk. Also gave French 'boulevard', a street built on old city walls. It reached English by 1500s as bulwark. Used figuratively for any safeguard. Today, bulwark means a defensive wall; a person or thing that protects.
