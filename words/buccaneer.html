        Where does the word "buccaneer" come from? It comes from Tupi mocaem, passing through French before entering English (1690s).

        1. mocaem (Tupi, pre-1500s): "a wooden meat-smoking frame". named the wooden frame used to smoke and preserve meat
        2. boucan (French, 1600s): "a wooden frame for smoking meat". French settlers in the Caribbean adopted the word for the same smoking technique
        3. boucanier (French, 1660s): "a meat-smoker, later a pirate". applied to hunters who smoked meat this way, many of whom turned to piracy
        4. buccaneer (English, 1690s): "a pirate, especially one who raided Spanish ships in the Caribbean". anglicized from French to describe Caribbean pirates

        Buccaneer’s story begins in Tupi: mocaem meant “a wooden meat-smoking frame.” Named the wooden frame used to smoke and preserve meat. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as boucan. French settlers in the Caribbean adopted the word for the same smoking technique. By 1660s, French speakers had reshaped it into boucanier. Applied to hunters who smoked meat this way, many of whom turned to piracy. It reached English by 1690s as buccaneer. Anglicized from French to describe Caribbean pirates. Today, buccaneer means a pirate or lawless adventurer, especially historically in the Caribbean.
