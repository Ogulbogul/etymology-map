Read the full story

Buccaneer’s story begins in Tupi: mocaem meant “a wooden meat-smoking frame.” Named the wooden frame used to smoke and preserve meat. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as boucan. French settlers in the Caribbean adopted the word for the same smoking technique. From there it passed into French as boucanier by 1660s. Applied to hunters who smoked meat this way, many of whom turned to piracy. It reached English by 1690s as buccaneer. Anglicized from French to describe Caribbean pirates. Today, buccaneer means a pirate or lawless adventurer, especially historically in the Caribbean.
