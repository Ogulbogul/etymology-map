Read the full story

Zill’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: zil meant “cymbal.” Cymbals; in 1623 Avedis, an Armenian alchemist in Constantinople, began casting them and was given the name 'Zilciyan', cymbal-maker. It reached English by 1900s as zill. The family firm, as Zildjian, moved to Massachusetts in 1929; 'zills' became the English name for dancers' finger cymbals. Today, zill means small finger cymbals played in pairs, especially by belly dancers.
