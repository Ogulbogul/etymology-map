Read the full story

Snack’s story begins in Dutch: snacken meant “to snap, bite.” An imitative word for the snap of teeth biting. By c. 1300, the word had taken hold in Middle English as snacke. Borrowed as a verb meaning to snap or bite at something. It reached English by mid-1700s onward as snack. Shifted from the act of biting to the small bite of food itself. Today, snack means a small amount of food eaten between main meals.
