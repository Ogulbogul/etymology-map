Read the full story

Syllable’s story begins in Greek: syllabē meant “what is held together.” Greek, 'syn', together, plus 'lambanein', to take. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as sillabe. Anglo-French added an extra 'l' to the older 'sillabe'. It reached English by 1300s as syllable. Used by grammarians for each beat of a spoken word. Today, syllable means a unit of pronunciation.
