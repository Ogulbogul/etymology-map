Read the full story

Duke’s story begins in Latin: dux meant “a leader, a general.” A leader, a general, from ducere (to lead). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as duc. Settled in French as a noble title just below a prince. It reached English by 1200s as duke. Settled as the English word for a high-ranking nobleman. Today, duke means a nobleman of the highest hereditary rank, below a prince.
