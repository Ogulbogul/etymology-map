Read the full story

Wood’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: widuz meant “tree, wood.” Related to Irish 'fid', tree. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as wudu. Meant both a forest and timber; place names like Woodstock and Wootton record woodland settlements. It reached English by 1500s as wood. 'knock on wood' is recorded from the early 1900s. Today, wood means the hard material of trees; an area of trees.
