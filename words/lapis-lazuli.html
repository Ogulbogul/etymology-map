Read the full story

Lapis lazuli’s story begins in Persian: lazhward meant “blue, azure.” A place name in Turkestan associated with the blue stone; also blue, azure. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as lazuward. Adapted from the Persian place name for the deep blue stone. From there it passed into Medieval Latin as lazulum by medieval. Adapted from Arabic for the deep blue stone. Medieval Latin picked it up next, as lapis lazuli, around medieval. Combined with lapis (stone) to mean the stone of azure. It reached English by 1400s as lapis lazuli. Adopted directly for the deep blue semi-precious stone. Today, lapis lazuli means a deep blue semi-precious stone, prized since antiquity and ground for pigment.
