Read the full story

Raki’s story begins in Arabic: 'araq meant “distilled spirit.” Literally "sweat," describing the condensing droplets of distillation. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Ottoman Turkish as raki. Formed from 'araq plus a relational suffix, becoming the generic Ottoman word for distilled spirits. It reached English by 1610s-20s as raki. Entered English describing the anise-flavored national spirit of Turkey and the Balkans. Today, raki means a strong, anise-flavored alcoholic spirit popular in Turkey, Greece, and the Balkans.
