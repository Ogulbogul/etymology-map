Read the full story

Introvert’s story begins in Latin: intro meant “within, inward.” Within, inward, combined with vertere (to turn). By 1600s, the word had taken hold in New Latin as introvertere. Formed for a mind turned inward, toward oneself. It reached English by 1900s as introvert. Carl Jung introduced it in his 1921 'Psychological Types'. Today, introvert means a reserved person who is more focused on their own thoughts than external interaction.
