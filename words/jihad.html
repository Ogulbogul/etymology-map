Read the full story

Jihad’s story begins in Arabic: jahada meant “to strive, to struggle.” Meant "he strove, he exerted effort". Within Arabic, it grew into jihad by medieval. Literally "struggle, effort," usually translated as "holy war". It reached English by 1852 as jihad. Entered English initially for religious war, later broadened figuratively to any zealous campaign. Today, jihad means a struggle or campaign for a cause, especially a religious duty in Islam, sometimes referring to armed conflict.
