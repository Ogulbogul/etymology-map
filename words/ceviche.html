Read the full story

Ceviche’s story begins in Quechua: siwichi meant “fresh fish.” Possibly meaning fresh fish, describing raw fish cured in citrus juice. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as ceviche. Peru, where it is a national dish, has a national Ceviche Day on 28 June. It reached English by 1900s as ceviche. Recorded in English from the early 1950s; the Spanish word is first documented in 1820 in the Peruvian patriotic song 'La Chicha'. Today, ceviche means a dish of raw fish or seafood cured in citrus juice.
