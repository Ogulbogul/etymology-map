Read the full story

Samba’s story begins in Kimbundu: semba meant “a navel-touching dance movement.” A term for a traditional Angolan dance movement. By 1800s-1900s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as samba. Developed in Brazil among Afro-Brazilian communities into a music and dance genre. It reached English by 1900s as samba. Rio de Janeiro's samba schools parade at Carnival; 'Pelo Telefone' (1917) was the first recorded samba. Today, samba means a lively Brazilian dance and music style, closely associated with Carnival.
