Imam bayildi’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: imam bayıldı meant “the imam fainted.” 'the imam fainted', from a tale of an imam who swooned at its taste, or at the cost of the olive oil. It reached English by 1900s as imam bayildi. Kept untranslated in English cookbooks of Middle Eastern food. Today, imam bayildi means a Turkish dish of aubergine stuffed with onion, garlic and tomato, cooked in olive oil.
