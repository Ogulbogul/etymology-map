Read the full story

Conceive’s story begins in Latin: concipere meant “to take together, to grasp.” To take together, to grasp, from com- (together) and capere (to take). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as concevoir. Carried into French for forming an idea or becoming pregnant. It reached English by 1300s as conceive. Settled as the English word for forming an idea in the mind, or becoming pregnant. Today, conceive means to form an idea or plan in the mind, or to become pregnant.
