Penguin’s story begins in Welsh: pen gwyn meant “white head.” 'white head', a long-proposed but disputed source (the great auk it first named had a black head with white patches); Latin pinguis 'fat' is another guess. It reached English by 1570s as penguin. First used for the great auk of the North Atlantic; Francis Drake's crew ate 'penguins' near the Strait of Magellan in 1578, moving the name south, and the great auk was hunted to extinction in 1844. Today, penguin means a flightless black-and-white seabird of the Southern Hemisphere.
