Read the full story

Imprint’s story begins in Latin: premere meant “to press.” To press, combined with in- (into). Within Latin, it grew into imprimere by ancient. To press into, from in- and premere. From there it passed into Old French as empreinte by medieval. Carried into French for a mark pressed into a surface. It reached English by 1400s as imprint. Settled as the English word for a mark made by pressing, or a lasting effect. Today, imprint means a mark made by pressing, or a lasting effect left on someone's mind or character.
