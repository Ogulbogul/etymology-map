Read the full story

Treacle’s story begins in Greek: thēriakḗ meant “antidote.” 'antidote to venomous bites', from thērion 'wild beast'; the physician Galen prepared theriac for Emperor Marcus Aurelius to take daily. By classical, the word had taken hold in Latin as theriaca. A complex remedy of dozens of ingredients, bound with lots of honey. From there it passed into Old French as triacle by 1200s. A cure-all medicine; 'Venice treacle' was a famous imported brand. It reached English by 1600s as treacle. The sweet syrup that carried the medicine gave its name to the syrup left from sugar refining. Today, treacle means thick sticky syrup from refining sugar; cloying sentimentality.
