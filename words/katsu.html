Read the full story

Katsu’s story begins in English: cutlet meant “a thin, breaded slice of meat.” The English word for a thin slice of breaded, fried meat. By Meiji era, the word had taken hold in Japanese as katsuretsu. Borrowed and adapted into a breaded, deep-fried cutlet dish, then clipped to 'katsu'. It reached English by 2000s-2010s as katsu. Re-imported into English as Japanese-style curry and cutlet dishes spread abroad. Today, katsu means a Japanese-style breaded and deep-fried cutlet, typically pork or chicken.
