Read the full story

Source’s story begins in Latin: surgere meant “to rise.” 'sub-', from below, plus 'regere', to direct. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as sourse. The rising point of a river. It reached English by 1300s as source. The sense 'origin of information' came in the 1700s. Today, source means a place or person from which something comes.
