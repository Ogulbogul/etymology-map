Read the full story

Obdurate’s story begins in Latin: durus meant “hard.” Hard, giving rise to durare (to harden). Within Latin, it grew into obdurare by ancient. To harden thoroughly, to persist stubbornly, from ob- and durare. It reached English by 1400s as obdurate. Settled as the English word for someone stubbornly refusing to change their mind. Today, obdurate means stubbornly refusing to change one's opinion, course of action, or feelings.
