Read the full story

Sang-froid’s story begins in Latin: sanguis + frigidus meant “cold blood.” 'blood' and 'cold' combined. By 1600s-1700s, the word had taken hold in French as sang froid. Literally 'cool blood', a metaphor for emotional composure under pressure. It reached English by 1712 as sang-froid. Borrowed whole for unflappable presence of mind. Today, sang-froid means composure or coolness, especially in trying circumstances.
