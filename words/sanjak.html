Read the full story

Sanjak’s story begins in Old Turkic: sanjaq meant “banner.” Carried as the sign of a military unit, as in other steppe armies. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in Ottoman Turkish as sancak. Provinces were divided into sanjaks, each under a 'sanjak-bey' who held the sultan's banner. It reached English by 1500s as sanjak. The Sandžak region between Serbia and Montenegro still bears the name of the Sanjak of Novi Pazar, Ottoman until 1912. Today, sanjak means an administrative district of the Ottoman Empire.
