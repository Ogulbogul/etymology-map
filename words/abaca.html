Read the full story

Abaca’s story begins in Tagalog/Bikol: abaka meant “a fiber-producing plant.” A banana-family plant whose leaf fiber is woven into rope and fabric. It reached English by 1800s as abaca. Exported from the Spanish Philippines through Manila, it became the world's main rope fibre for 19th-century sailing ships. Today, abaca means a strong fiber from a banana-family plant, used for rope and fabric, also called Manila hemp.
