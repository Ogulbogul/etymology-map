Read the full story

Nixtamal’s story begins in Nahuatl: nextamalli meant “lime-treated corn dough.” Combined nextli 'ashes/lime' and tamalli 'dough', describing corn soaked in lime water. By 1500s-1600s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as nixtamal. Nahuatl 'ashes' plus 'dough'; soaking maize in lime frees niacin and prevents pellagra. It reached English by 1950s as nixtamalization. Today, nixtamal means the traditional process of soaking and cooking maize in an alkaline solution to make it more nutritious and easier to grind.
