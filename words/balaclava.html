Read the full story

Balaclava’s story begins in Crimean Tatar: Balaklava meant “a Crimean port.” A harbour town near Sevastopol, known to the Genoese as Cembalo; the name is often explained as Turkic balık 'fish' + yuva 'nest', though this is disputed. It reached English by 1880s as balaclava. The Battle of Balaclava in October 1854 included the Charge of the Light Brigade; knitted head-coverings sent to freezing British troops in Crimea later took the name. Today, balaclava means a close-fitting woollen hood covering the head and neck.
