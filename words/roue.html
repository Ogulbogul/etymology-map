Read the full story

Roue’s story begins in Latin: rotare meant “to roll, turn.” The verb of rolling or turning. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in French as rouer. Developed the grim sense of breaking a condemned man on a wheel. By 1720, French speakers had reshaped it into roue. The past participle was applied, half-ironically, to dissolute friends of the Duke of Orleans who 'deserved' such punishment. It reached English by 1800 as roue. Borrowed for a man devoted to a life of sensual excess. Today, roue means a debauched man devoted to a life of sensual pleasure; a rake.
