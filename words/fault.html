Read the full story

Fault’s story begins in Vulgar Latin: fallere meant “to deceive, fail.” The root of 'false' and 'fail'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as faute. The 'l' was restored in English spelling in the 1400s and later pronounced. It reached English by 1300s as fault. Geological 'faults' date from the 1790s. Today, fault means a defect; responsibility for something wrong.
