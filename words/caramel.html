Read the full story

Caramel’s story begins in Medieval Latin: cannamellis meant “sugar cane.” Possibly combining canna (cane) and mellis (honey), though the origin is uncertain. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old Spanish as caramel. Of uncertain origin, came to mean burnt sugar. From there it passed into French as caramel by 1600s-1700s. Adopted from Spanish for the confection and its flavor. It reached English by 1725 as caramel. Borrowed directly from French. Today, caramel means a chewy candy or flavoring made from heated sugar.
