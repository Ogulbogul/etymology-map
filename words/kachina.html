Read the full story

Kachina’s story begins in Hopi: katsina meant “a spirit being that links gods and mortals.” Named the supernatural spirit beings central to Hopi religious life. It reached English by 1880s as kachina. Anglicized by ethnographers and later popularized through carved kachina dolls sold to collectors. Today, kachina means in Hopi and other Pueblo traditions, a spirit being, or a carved wooden doll representing one.
