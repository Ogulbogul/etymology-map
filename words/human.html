Read the full story

Human’s story begins in Latin: humanus meant “of people; kind.” Related to homo 'man' and humus 'earth'; Terence's comedy of 163 BC has the line 'Homo sum, humani nihil a me alienum puto', I am human, nothing human is alien to me. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as humain. Meant both 'of people' and 'kindly'. It reached English by 1300s as human. 'human' and 'humane' were one word until the 1700s, when the spellings split along with the meanings. Today, human means relating to people; a person.
