Read the full story

Mutton’s story begins in Gaulish (Celtic): multo meant “sheep.” A Celtic word for sheep, current in Gaul before and during Roman rule. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Medieval Latin as multonem. The Gallo-Roman Latin form of the Celtic word, source of Old French moton. From there it passed into Old French as moton by medieval. Used for both the sheep and its meat. It reached English by medieval as mutton. Adopted for the meat, while "sheep" stayed the native English word for the animal. Today, mutton means the meat of a mature sheep.
