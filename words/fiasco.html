Read the full story

Fiasco’s story begins in Late Latin: flasco meant “a bottle.” Meaning "a bottle" or "flask". By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Italian as fiasco. From the theatrical phrase "far fiasco" (to make a bottle), meaning to fail on stage. From there it passed into French as faire fiasco by 1800s. Adopted the Italian theatrical phrase to mean "turn out a failure". It reached English by 1855 as fiasco. Adopted via French theatrical slang for an embarrassing and complete failure. Today, fiasco means a complete and embarrassing failure.
