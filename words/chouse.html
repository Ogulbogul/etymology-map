Read the full story

Chouse’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: çavuş meant “messenger, envoy.” A palace messenger or envoy; in the modern Turkish army it means sergeant. It reached English by 1610s as chouse. First 'chiaus', a Turkish envoy; in 1609 an interpreter sent to London by Sir Robert Shirley swindled merchants of £4,000, and Jonson's 'The Alchemist' (1610) jokes about it. Today, chouse means to cheat or swindle.
