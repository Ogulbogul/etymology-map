Read the full story

Loan’s story begins in Old Norse: lán meant “that which is lent; a gift from a superior.” Brought by Viking settlers, it displaced the native Old English cognate 'læn'. By late 1100s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as lone. Replaced Old English 'lǣn', which survives in 'lend'. It reached English by 1540s onward as loan. Gained a verb use and settled into its modern financial sense. Today, loan means a sum of money or item lent, to be returned or repaid.
