Read the full story

Tapioca’s story begins in Tupi-Guarani: typyoca meant “starch sediment from cassava.” Referring to the starchy sediment squeezed from cassava root. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as tapioca. From Tupi 'tipi'óka', starch squeezed out. It reached English by 1700s as tapioca. Today, tapioca means a starchy substance made from cassava root, used in puddings and drinks.
