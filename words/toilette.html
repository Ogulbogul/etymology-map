Read the full story

Toilette’s story begins in Latin: tela meant “web, cloth.” From texere 'to weave' - a word for woven cloth. By medieval, the word had taken hold in French as toile. Kept the sense of cloth or fabric. By 1500s, French speakers had reshaped it into toilette. A diminutive of toile, first for a cloth cover on a dressing table, then for the act of dressing itself. It reached English by 1530s-1819 as toilette. Entered for a cloth wrapper, then widened through 'act of dressing' to a dressing room, and eventually a bathroom. Today, toilette means the process of washing and dressing, or informally, a bathroom.
