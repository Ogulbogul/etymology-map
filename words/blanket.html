Read the full story

Blanket’s story begins in Frankish: blank meant “white, bright.” A Germanic word for shining or white, also the source of 'blank'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as blanquette. Undyed white woollen cloth. It reached English by 1300s as blanket. The adjective 'blanket', covering everything, dates from the 1800s. Today, blanket means a large piece of warm cloth used as a bed covering; covering all cases.
