Read the full story

Violin’s story begins in Medieval Latin: vitula meant “a stringed instrument, or joy.” Possibly related to a goddess of joy and celebration, or to a stringed instrument. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Italian as violino. A diminutive form settled in Italian for the small, high-pitched bowed instrument. It reached English by 1570s as violin. Adopted directly for the four-stringed bowed instrument. Today, violin means a four-stringed musical instrument played with a bow.
