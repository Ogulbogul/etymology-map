Read the full story

Marinate’s story begins in Latin: marinus meant “of the sea.” Of the sea, from mare (sea), since food was originally soaked in seawater or brine. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as marinare. Settled in Italian for soaking food in a seasoned liquid. It reached English by 1640s as marinate. Settled as the English word for soaking food in a flavorful liquid before cooking. Today, marinate means to soak food in a seasoned liquid before cooking, to add flavor or tenderize it.
