Read the full story

Spahi’s story begins in Persian: sipāhī meant “soldier.” From 'sipāh', army; the same word reached English as 'sepoy' through India. By 1300s-1600s, the word had taken hold in Ottoman Turkish as sipahi. Cavalrymen paid with 'timar' land grants instead of wages, the backbone of early Ottoman armies. From there it passed into French as spahi by 1830s. France raised Algerian spahi cavalry regiments from 1834, known for their red cloaks. It reached English by 1560s as spahi. First used for Ottoman horsemen, later for France's North African cavalry. Today, spahi means an Ottoman cavalryman; later a North African cavalryman in the French army.
