Read the full story

Harlequin’s story begins in Italian: Arlecchino meant “a comic servant character in Italian theater.” A stock bumbling-servant character in commedia dell'arte, of disputed deeper origin. By 1580s, the word had taken hold in English as harlequin. Borrowed for the same stock theatrical character. It reached English by 1779 as harlequin. Extended to a diamond-patterned, multicolored design, after the character's costume. Today, harlequin means a diamond-patterned, multicolored design; historically, a comic pantomime character.
