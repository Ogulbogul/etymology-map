Read the full story

Lewd’s story begins in Old English: lǣwede meant “lay, unlearned.” 'lay, not of the clergy', perhaps from Latin laicus 'lay'. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as lewed. 'ignorant, common'; Chaucer's Parson reasons that 'if a preest be foul, on whom we truste, no wonder is a lewed man to ruste'. It reached English by 1500s as lewd. Sank through 'vile, base' to 'lustful, obscene'. Today, lewd means crude and offensive in a sexual way.
