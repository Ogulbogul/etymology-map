Read the full story

Truant’s story begins in Gaulish: *trugant- meant “wretch.” A likely Celtic source, reconstructed from its relatives Welsh truan and Old Irish trúag, both 'wretched'. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Old French as truand. 'beggar, vagabond'; Paris still has a rue de la Grande-Truanderie in Les Halles, named after them. It reached English by 1300s as truant. Shifted from 'idle beggar' to 'pupil who skips school'; attendance became compulsory in England with the Elementary Education Act of 1880. Today, truant means a pupil who stays away from school without permission.
