Read the full story

Skate’s story begins in Dutch: schaats meant “an ice skate.” The Dutch word for a blade attached to the foot for gliding on ice. It reached English by 1600s as skate. Dutch 'schaats', ice skate; English exiles in Holland brought skating home in the 1660s. Today, skate means a boot fitted with a blade or wheels, used for gliding over ice or ground.
