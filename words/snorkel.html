Read the full story

Snorkel’s story begins in German: schnorcheln meant “to snore, snuffle.” From Low German dialect words for snoring or snuffling, describing a rasping sound. By 1944, the word had taken hold in German as Schnorchel. German navy slang for the WWII U-boat airshaft, named for its snout-like shape and noise. It reached English by 1944 onward as snorkel. Adopted by Allied forces, later applied to a swimmer's breathing tube. Today, snorkel means a tube used for breathing while swimming face-down at the surface.
