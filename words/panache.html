Read the full story

Panache’s story begins in Late Latin: pinnaculum meant “a small wing or peak.” "small wing, gable, peak", from Latin pinna (feather, point). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as pennacchio. A plume of feathers worn on a helmet. From there it passed into French as pennache by medieval. The French form of the Italian plume word. It reached English by 1500s as panache. Broadened from the literal plume to flamboyant confidence and style. Today, panache means flamboyant confidence of style or manner.
