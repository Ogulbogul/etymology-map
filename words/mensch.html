Read the full story

Mensch’s story begins in German: Mensch meant “a human being.” Meaning "a human being" or "person". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Yiddish as mentsh. Narrowed to mean a person of integrity and honor. It reached English by 1900s as mensch. Adopted through Yiddish-speaking immigrant communities. Today, mensch means a person of integrity and honor, someone admirable and decent.
