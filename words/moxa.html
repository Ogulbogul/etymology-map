Read the full story

Moxa’s story begins in Japanese: mogusa meant “dried mugwort used for burning treatments.” The native name for dried mugwort burned on the skin in traditional medicine. By 1670s, the word had taken hold in Dutch as moxa. Shortened and carried into European medical writing by a Dutch minister describing the remedy from Japan. It reached English by 1675 as moxa. Adopted into English medical vocabulary soon after the Dutch account appeared. Today, moxa means dried mugwort burned on or near the skin in the traditional therapy called moxibustion.
