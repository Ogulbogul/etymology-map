Read the full story

Mask’s story begins in Arabic: maskhara meant “a jester, a disguised person.” A buffoon or someone in disguise for mockery. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as maschera. Carried into Italian for a face covering worn in performance. From there it passed into French as masque by 1500s. A covering to hide or guard the face. It reached English by 1530s as mask. Settled as the English word for a face covering. Today, mask means a covering worn over the face for protection, disguise, or performance.
