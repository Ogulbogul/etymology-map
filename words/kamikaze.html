Read the full story

Kamikaze’s story begins in Japanese: kami + kaze meant “divine wind.” Literally "divine wind", a typhoon believed to have destroyed an invading fleet. By 1944, the word had taken hold in Japanese as kamikaze. Revived as the name for suicide attack pilots in World War II. It reached English by 1940s as kamikaze. Adopted during World War II, later used more loosely. Today, kamikaze means a suicide attack, or (loosely) a reckless, self-destructive act.
