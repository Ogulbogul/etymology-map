Read the full story

Manitou’s story begins in Proto-Algonquian: manetowa meant “spirit, sacred power.” Named the spiritual force or power present in people, animals, and places. By 1670s, the word had taken hold in French as manitou. Recorded by French missionaries and traders among Great Lakes Algonquian peoples. It reached English by 1670s-1800s as manitou. Borrowed from French accounts of Great Lakes indigenous religion. Today, manitou means a spiritual force or being central to Algonquian traditional religion, ranging from minor spirits to the 'Great Spirit'.
