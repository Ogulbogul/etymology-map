Read the full story

Capable’s story begins in Latin: capere meant “to take, to hold.” To take, to hold. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as capax. Able to hold much, from capere. From there it passed into French as capable by 1500s. Carried into French for having the ability to do something. It reached English by 1580s as capable. Settled as the English word for having the ability or capacity to do something. Today, capable means having the ability, skill, or capacity to do something.
