Read the full story

Penicillin’s story begins in Latin: penicillus meant “paintbrush.” 'a little tail, a painter's brush', diminutive of penis 'tail'; also the source of 'pencil'. By 1809, the word had taken hold in New Latin as Penicillium. The botanist Johann Link named this mould genus in 1809 for its brush-like spore stalks. It reached English by 1929 as penicillin. Alexander Fleming noticed in 1928 that a stray mould killed bacteria on a culture plate at St Mary's Hospital, London, and named its active substance in 1929; Howard Florey and Ernst Chain's Oxford team made it a usable drug from 1940. Today, penicillin means an antibiotic produced from Penicillium moulds.
