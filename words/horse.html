Read the full story

Horse’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: hrussą meant “horse.” Unlike Latin 'equus' or Greek 'hippos', this root is unrelated to the shared Indo-European word for horse, and its ultimate origin remains unknown. By old english, the word had taken hold in Old English as hors. A neuter noun whose plural stayed identical to the singular, much like modern 'sheep' or 'deer'. It reached English by medieval as horse. James Watt coined 'horsepower' in the 1780s to market his steam engines by comparing them to a horse's pulling power. Today, horse means a large, solid-hoofed mammal used for riding, racing, and pulling loads.
