Read the full story

Shampoo’s story begins in Sanskrit: capayati meant “to press, to knead.” Meaning "pounds, kneads"; root of the Hindi imperative champo. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in Hindi as chāmpo. From "chāmpnā", to press or knead the body. From there it passed into English as shampoo by 1762. British visitors adopted the practice and word from Indian bathhouses. It reached English by 1860s as shampoo. Narrowed over time to mean washing the hair specifically. Today, shampoo means a liquid or cream product used to wash hair.
