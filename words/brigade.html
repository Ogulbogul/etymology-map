Read the full story

Brigade’s story begins in Italian: briga meant “strife, conflict.” Meaning "strife" or "a fight". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as brigata. Came to mean a company or troop banded together. From there it passed into French as brigade by 1600s. Adopted for a large body of troops. It reached English by 1600s-1700s as brigade. Adopted directly from French. Today, brigade means a large military unit, or an organized group performing a task together.
