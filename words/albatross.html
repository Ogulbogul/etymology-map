Read the full story

Albatross’s story begins in Arabic: al-ghaṭṭās meant “the diver.” 'the diver', used for sea birds. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as alcatraz. A pelican or frigate bird; San Francisco's Alcatraz Island is named after the Spanish form. It reached English by 1670s as albatross. Altered by Latin 'albus', white; Coleridge's 'Ancient Mariner' of 1798 made the albatross a burden of guilt. Today, albatross means a very large oceanic bird; a burden.
