Read the full story

Algorithm’s story begins in Arabic: al-Khwarizmi meant “native of Khwarazm; a mathematician's surname.” Literally native of Khwarazm, the surname of the mathematician Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, whose Arabic-language treatises introduced advanced calculation methods to the West. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Medieval Latin as algorismus. A mangled transliteration of al-Khwarizmi, entering Europe through Latin translations of Arabic mathematical texts made in Spain. From there it passed into Old French as algorisme by 1200s. Carried into French for the Arabic system of calculation, later reshaped to "algorithme" by false association with Greek arithmos (number). It reached English by 1690s as algorithm. Adopted from French algorithme for a step-by-step procedure for calculations. Today, algorithm means a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem or performing a computation.
