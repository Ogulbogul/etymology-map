Read the full story

Improvise’s story begins in Latin: improvisus meant “unforeseen.” 'in-', not, plus 'provisus', foreseen. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Italian as improvvisare. Came via Italian and then French 'improviser'. It reached English by 1820s as improvise. Improv comedy dates from the 1900s. Today, improvise means to create without preparation.
