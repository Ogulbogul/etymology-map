Read the full story

Manifesto’s story begins in Latin: manifestus meant “clear, evident, obvious.” Manus 'hand' combined with -festus, suggesting something 'caught by hand', hence plainly evident. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as manifesto. Used as a noun for proof or clear evidence. It reached English by 1640s as manifesto. Borrowed for a public declaration explaining past actions and future intent. Today, manifesto means a public declaration of policy and aims, especially political.
