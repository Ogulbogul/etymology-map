Read the full story

Cachet’s story begins in Old French: cacher meant “to press.” To press, later to hide. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as cachet. A seal; a royal 'lettre de cachet' could send someone to the Bastille without trial. It reached English by 1630s as cachet. From a seal of approval it came to mean prestige, by the 1840s. Today, cachet means prestige; a distinctive mark of quality.
