Read the full story

Lacquer’s story begins in Persian: lak meant “lac resin.” The resinous substance secreted by the lac insect. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as lakk. Adapted from Persian for the resinous substance. From there it passed into Portuguese as lacca by 1500s. From 'lac', the resin secreted by lac insects; Portuguese traders brought Asian lacquerware to Europe. French picked it up next, as lacre, around 1500s. A kind of sealing wax, an unexplained variant of the Portuguese term. It reached English by 1570s as lacquer. Settled as the English word for the glossy varnish. Today, lacquer means a glossy, protective coating applied to wood or metal surfaces.
