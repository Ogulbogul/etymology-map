Read the full story

Hurricane’s story begins in Taíno: hurakán meant “storm god.” Named after a storm deity of the Caribbean Taíno people. By c. 1500s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as huracán. Recorded by Spanish colonizers encountering Caribbean storms. It reached English by c. 1550s as hurricane. Spelling settled after passing through several variants. Today, hurricane means a severe tropical cyclone with violent winds.
