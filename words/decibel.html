Read the full story

Decibel’s story begins in Scots: Bell meant “a family name.” Alexander Graham Bell, born in Edinburgh in 1847, patented the telephone in 1876. It reached English by 1928 as decibel. Latin decimus 'tenth' + bel; Bell Telephone Laboratories named the bel after him in 1928, and the tenth-part decibel became the practical unit. Today, decibel means a unit measuring the intensity of sound.
