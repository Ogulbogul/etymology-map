Read the full story

Ditto’s story begins in Latin: dicere meant “to speak, say.” The verb of speaking, whose past participle was dictus. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as detto. The past participle of dire, 'said'. From there it passed into Tuscan Italian as ditto by 1600s. Used by merchants to mean '(in) the said month', avoiding repetition in dated ledgers. It reached English by 1620s-1670s as ditto. Entered for the same bookkeeping shorthand, then generalized to mean 'the same as stated'. Today, ditto means used to avoid repeating a word, meaning 'the same as stated'.
