Read the full story

Quinine’s story begins in Quechua: kina meant “bark, specifically fever-tree bark.” Named the bark of the cinchona tree used to treat fevers. By 1630s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as quina. Adopted by Spanish Jesuits who learned of the fever-bark remedy in Peru. From there it passed into French as quinine by 1820. Coined by French chemists Pelletier and Caventou when they isolated the active alkaloid. It reached English by 1820s as quinine. Borrowed directly from the French scientific term. Today, quinine means a bitter alkaloid derived from cinchona bark, historically used to treat malaria and to flavor tonic water.
