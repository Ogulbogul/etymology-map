Read the full story

Gendarme’s story begins in Latin: gens + arma meant “men at arms.” 'people, nation' and 'weapons' combined. By 1300s, the word had taken hold in Old French as gens d'armes. A compound phrase for armed troops. From there it passed into French as gendarme by 1540s-1790s. Contracted into a single word for a mounted trooper, then formalized as the name of France's military police. It reached English by 1796 as gendarme. Borrowed for the French police force. Today, gendarme means a member of the French police force, especially one with military-style organization.
