Read the full story

Chair’s story begins in Greek: kathedra meant “seat.” 'kata', down, plus 'hedra', seat; a bishop's throne gave 'cathedral'. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as cathedra. A chair of authority, especially a teacher's. From there it passed into Old French as chaere by medieval. The 'th' sound wore away; a parallel form became French 'chaise'. It reached English by 1200s as chair. Also names the person presiding over a meeting, from where they sat. Today, chair means a seat for one person with a back; the person leading a meeting.
