Read the full story

Pastry’s story begins in Late Latin: pasta meant “dough, paste.” From Greek 'pastē', a barley porridge sprinkled with salt. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as paste. The dough bakers worked with; the same word later gave 'pâté' and 'pâtisserie'. It reached English by 1530s as pastry. Formed in English from 'paste' plus -ry, on the model of 'pantry'. Today, pastry means a dough of flour, fat and water; baked goods made from it.
