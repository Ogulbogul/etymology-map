Read the full story

Maraca’s story begins in Tupi: maraka meant “a hollow rattle instrument.” Named the hollow, seed-filled rattle used in ceremonies. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Portuguese as maracá. Tupi shamans used gourd rattles in ceremonies. It reached English by 1650s as maraca. Borrowed into English through accounts of South American music and culture. Today, maraca means a hand-held percussion instrument consisting of a hollow shell filled with beads or seeds.
