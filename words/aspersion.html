Read the full story

Aspersion’s story begins in Latin: spargere meant “to scatter, to sprinkle.” To scatter, to sprinkle, combined with ad- (toward). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as aspergere. To sprinkle on, from ad- and spargere. From there it passed into Latin as aspersio by ancient. A sprinkling, later a damaging remark spread about someone, from aspergere. It reached English by 1500s as aspersion. Settled as the English word for a critical or damaging remark about someone. Today, aspersion means a critical or damaging remark that questions someone's character or reputation.
