Read the full story

Madrigal’s story begins in Latin: matrix meant “womb.” A word for a womb, the place of origin. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Late Latin as matricalis. An adjective meaning original or invented, derived from the womb-word. From there it passed into Venetian Italian as madregal by medieval. A dialect form describing something simple and artless. Italian picked it up next, as madrigale, around medieval. Named a short love poem, later a part-song for several voices. It reached English by 1580s as madrigal. Borrowed during the Renaissance vogue for Italian musical forms. Today, madrigal means a part-song for several voices, especially of the 16th and 17th centuries.
