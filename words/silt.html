Read the full story

Silt’s story begins in Scandinavian: sylt meant “salt marsh.” Probably Norse; Danish and Norwegian 'sylt' mean a salt marsh, related to 'salt'. By 1400s, the word had taken hold in Middle English as sylte. Sediment left in harbours and rivers. It reached English by 1500s as silt. Silting harbours ruined medieval ports such as Chester. Today, silt means fine sand or clay carried by running water.
