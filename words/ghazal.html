Read the full story

Ghazal’s story begins in Arabic: ghazal meant “to flirt, love-talk.” Meant "to sweet-talk, to flirt," related to ghazaal, "young gazelle". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as ghazal. Developed into a lyric poetic form of rhyming couplets on love and loss. From there it passed into Urdu/Hindi as ghazal by 1600s. Flourished further as a major poetic form in Mughal India. It reached English by 1800s as ghazal. Entered English literary criticism and was later adopted by English-language poets. Today, ghazal means a lyric poem made of rhyming couplets, traditionally on themes of love and loss, originating in Arabic and developed in Persian and Urdu literature.
