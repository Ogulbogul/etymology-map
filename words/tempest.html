Read the full story

Tempest’s story begins in Latin: tempestas meant “a period of weather, a season.” A season, a period of weather, from tempus (time). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as tempeste. Narrowed in French to mean a violent storm. It reached English by 1200s as tempest. Settled as the English word for a violent windstorm. Today, tempest means a violent storm, or figuratively, a violent outburst of emotion.
