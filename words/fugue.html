Read the full story

Fugue’s story begins in Latin: fugere meant “to flee; a fleeing.” The verb of fleeing, giving the noun fuga. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as fuga. Kept the literal sense of flight. From there it passed into English as fuge by 1590s. Borrowed for a musical form where one melodic voice seems to flee and others pursue it. It reached English by 1660s as fugue. The spelling was refashioned after the French rendering of the Italian word. Today, fugue means a contrapuntal musical composition in which a theme is introduced and developed by imitation.
