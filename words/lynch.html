Read the full story

Lynch’s story begins in Irish: Lynch meant “a family name.” An Irish surname, from Ó Loingsigh or the Anglo-Norman Lynch family, one of the fourteen 'Tribes of Galway'. By 1780, the word had taken hold in American English as Lynch's law. Usually traced to Charles Lynch, a Virginia justice who ran extralegal courts punishing Loyalists in 1780, though Captain William Lynch of Pittsylvania County is a rival claimant. It reached English by 1830s as lynch. Hardened into mob killing; the Tuskegee Institute recorded about 4,700 lynchings in the US between 1882 and 1968, most of them of Black Americans. Today, lynch means (of a mob) to kill someone for an alleged offence without a legal trial.
