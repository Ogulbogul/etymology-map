Read the full story

Omelet’s story begins in Latin: lamella meant “thin plate.” A diminutive of 'lamina', thin layer. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in French as amelette. A thin flat egg dish, altered from 'alumelle', blade. It reached English by 1610s as omelet. 'you can't make an omelette without breaking eggs' is from French. Today, omelet means a dish of beaten eggs cooked flat.
