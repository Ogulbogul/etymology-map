Read the full story

Nickel’s story begins in German: kupfernickel meant “false copper, devil's copper.” Literally devil's copper, from Nickel (a mischievous sprite), blamed for ore that yielded no copper. By 1750s, the word had taken hold in Swedish as kopparnickel. Shortened by Swedish mineralogist Axel Cronstedt when he named the new metallic element. It reached English by 1750s as nickel. Settled as the name for the silvery-white metallic element. Today, nickel means a hard, silvery-white metallic element, or informally a five-cent coin.
