Read the full story

Sesame’s story begins in Akkadian: shamash-shammu meant “oil-seed.” Assyrian/Babylonian term literally meaning "oil-seed"; a very early borrowing reached Greek via Phoenician traders (Arabic simsim is a related Semitic cognate, not a direct ancestor). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Greek as sesamon. Borrowed from the Assyrian/Babylonian term for the seed. From there it passed into Latin as sesamum by ancient. Borrowed from Greek sesamon. Old French picked it up next, as sisamin, around medieval. From Medieval Latin sisaminum. It reached English by 1400s as sesame. Settled as the English word for the oil-rich seed. Today, sesame means a small edible seed used in cooking, famous from the phrase open sesame.
