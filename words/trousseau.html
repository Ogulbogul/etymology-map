Read the full story

Trousseau’s story begins in Old French: trousse meant “a bundle of belongings.” A bundle, a small pack of belongings. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as trousseau. Narrowed to mean a bride's collection of clothing and household linens. It reached English by 1817 as trousseau. Adopted directly for a bride's personal collection gathered before marriage. Today, trousseau means the clothes, linens, and other belongings collected by a bride for her marriage.
