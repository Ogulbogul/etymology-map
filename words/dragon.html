Read the full story

Dragon’s story begins in Greek: drákōn meant “serpent.” 'serpent', probably 'the one with the deadly glance', from derkesthai 'to see'; the Athenian lawgiver Draco (621 BC) gave us 'draconian'. By classical, the word had taken hold in Latin as draco. Roman cavalry adopted the draco, a hollow serpent-headed windsock standard, from Sarmatian and Dacian horsemen in the 100s AD. From there it passed into Old French as dragon by 1100s. The dragon-slaying St George became hugely popular in the West after the Crusades. It reached English by 1200s as dragon. The red dragon of Wales, Y Ddraig Goch, was officially recognised as the Welsh flag in 1959. Today, dragon means a mythical monster like a huge reptile, often breathing fire.
