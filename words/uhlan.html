Read the full story

Uhlan’s story begins in Tatar: oğlan meant “boy, youth.” In Turkic households and armies it also named a young page or soldier. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in Polish as ułan. Tatar light horsemen serving the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth gave their name to its lancer regiments. From there it passed into German as Uhlan by 1700s. Prussia and Austria formed uhlan regiments on the Polish model. It reached English by 1750s as uhlan. The Franco-Prussian War of 1870 made German uhlans notorious in British newspapers. Today, uhlan means a lancer in the Polish, and later German and Austrian, cavalry.
