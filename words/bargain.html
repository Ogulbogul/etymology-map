Read the full story

Bargain’s story begins in Frankish: borganjan meant “to lend, borrow.” A Germanic verb related to 'borrow'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as bargaignier. To haggle over terms in the marketplace. It reached English by 1300s as bargain. The sense 'something bought cheaply' came in the 1500s. Today, bargain means something bought for less than its usual price; an agreement.
