Read the full story

Matzo’s story begins in Hebrew: matzah meant “unleavened bread.” Unleavened bread eaten during Passover. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Yiddish as matse. Kept as the everyday word for the flatbread. It reached English by 1800s-1900s as matzo. Eaten at Passover to recall the Israelites' hurried flight from Egypt. Today, matzo means a flat, unleavened bread traditionally eaten during Passover.
