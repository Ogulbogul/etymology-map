Read the full story

Onerous’s story begins in Latin: onus meant “burden, load.” Burden, load. Within Latin, it grew into onerosus by ancient. Burdensome, from onus. From there it passed into Old French as onereus by medieval. Carried into French for something heavy or difficult to bear. It reached English by 1300s as onerous. Settled as the English word for something involving great effort or difficulty. Today, onerous means involving a great deal of effort, trouble, or difficulty.
