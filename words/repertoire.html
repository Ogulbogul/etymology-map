Read the full story

Repertoire’s story begins in Latin: reperire meant “to find, invent.” Re- (intensive) plus an old verb 'to produce, bring forth'. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Late Latin as repertorium. A noun formed for a catalogued list of things found. From there it passed into French as repertoire by 1300s. Narrowed to mean a running index or list. It reached English by 1847 as repertoire. Specialized to the stock of works a performer or company has ready to present. Today, repertoire means the range of skills, works, or techniques a person or group is prepared to perform.
