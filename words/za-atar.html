Za'atar’s story begins in Arabic: za'atar meant “wild thyme, or a spice blend.” The name for a wild thyme herb, and the spice blend made from it. It reached English by 1900s as za'atar. Arabic for wild thyme; Lebanese man'oushe flatbread is topped with za'atar and olive oil. Today, za'atar means a Middle Eastern spice blend made from thyme, sumac, and toasted sesame seeds.
