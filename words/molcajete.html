Read the full story

Molcajete’s story begins in Nahuatl: molcaxitl meant “a sauce bowl, mortar.” Combined 'molli' (sauce) and 'caxitl' (bowl), naming the stone mortar used to grind ingredients. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as molcajete. Adopted into Mexican Spanish for the traditional stone mortar and pestle. It reached English by 1900s-1910s as molcajete. Borrowed into English through Mexican culinary tradition. Today, molcajete means a traditional stone mortar and pestle used in Mexican cooking to grind spices and prepare salsa.
