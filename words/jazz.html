Read the full story

Jazz’s story begins in American English: jasm meant “pep, energy.” Slang for energy or spirit, the most likely source, though the origin of 'jazz' is genuinely unknown. Within American English, it grew into jazz by 1912. First printed in the Los Angeles Times in April 1912, when pitcher Ben Henderson described his 'jazz ball' that wobbled so no one could hit it. It reached English by 1917 as jazz. The name of the new New Orleans music by 1915; the Original Dixieland Jass Band made the first jazz record in New York in February 1917. Today, jazz means a style of music with African-American roots, marked by improvisation and syncopation.
