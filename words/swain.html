Read the full story

Swain’s story begins in Old Norse: sveinn meant “boy, servant.” A boy or servant. By 1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as swān. Borrowed from Norse settlers; 'boatswain' and 'coxswain' keep the sense of servant. It reached English by 1580s as swain. Pastoral poets made the country youth a lover. Today, swain means a young lover or suitor; a country youth.
