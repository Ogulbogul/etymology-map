Read the full story

Reconnaissance’s story begins in Latin: recognoscere meant “to recognize, know again.” Re- 'again' plus cognoscere 'to know'. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as reconoistre. Gave rise to a noun for the act of recognizing or acknowledging. From there it passed into French as reconnaissance by 1700s-1800s. Specialized during the Napoleonic Wars into a term for surveying enemy territory. It reached English by 1810 as reconnaissance. The Napoleonic Wars made it a standard military term. Today, reconnaissance means military observation of a region to gather information about enemy forces.
