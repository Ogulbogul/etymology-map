Read the full story

Church’s story begins in Greek: kyriakon dōma meant “the Lord's house.” 'of the Lord', from 'kyrios', lord. By ancient, the word had taken hold in West Germanic as kirika. Picked up by Germanic peoples along the Roman frontier, perhaps from Greek-speaking Christians. From there it passed into Old English as cirice by medieval. Scots kept the hard sound in 'kirk'. It reached English by 1200s as church. The 'k' sounds softened to 'ch' in southern English. Today, church means a building for Christian worship; a Christian organization.
