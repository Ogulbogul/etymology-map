Read the full story

Coach’s story begins in Hungarian: kocsi meant “a wagon from Kocs.” Literally "of Kocs", the village where the vehicle was built. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in German as Kutsche. The design spread across Central Europe. From there it passed into French as coche by 1500s. Adopted for the fashionable new carriages. It reached English by 1500s as coach. Later extended metaphorically to a personal trainer or tutor. Today, coach means a person who trains and instructs others, or a large road or rail vehicle.
