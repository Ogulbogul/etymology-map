Read the full story

Heresy’s story begins in Greek: hairesis meant “a choice, a chosen belief.” Originally simply a choice or a chosen course of action. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as heresie. Narrowed under church influence to mean forbidden belief. It reached English by 1200s as heresy. Settled as the word for belief contrary to accepted doctrine. Today, heresy means belief or opinion contrary to orthodox religious or accepted doctrine.
