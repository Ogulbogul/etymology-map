Read the full story

Gutta-percha’s story begins in Malay: getah perca meant “latex of the percha tree.” Getah 'sap, latex' plus perca, probably the tree's name; Malay workers moulded the hardened gum into knife handles. It reached English by 1840s as gutta-percha. William Montgomerie, a surgeon in Singapore, sent samples to London's Society of Arts in 1843; it insulated the first transatlantic telegraph cable in 1858 and made the 'guttie' golf ball. Today, gutta-percha means a hard rubbery latex from Malaysian trees, once used for cable insulation and dentistry.
