Read the full story

Squall’s story begins in Swedish: skvala meant “to gush, to cry out.” An imitative word for water gushing or pouring out noisily. It reached English by 1630s as squall. First a verb for crying out loudly, as a baby does; later, sailors applied it to a sudden, violent gust of wind and rain. Today, squall means a sudden violent gust of wind or storm; a loud cry.
