Read the full story

Infantry’s story begins in Latin: infans meant “an infant, a youth.” Literally unable to speak, an infant, extended to mean a young person or foot soldier. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as infanteria. Settled in Italian for young foot soldiers serving under a knight. It reached English by 1570s as infantry. Italian 'infante', youth, since foot soldiers were young men serving knights. Today, infantry means soldiers who fight on foot, forming the main body of an army.
