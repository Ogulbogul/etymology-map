Read the full story

Camas’s story begins in Nez Perce: qém̓es meant “sweet; the camas lily bulb.” Meant 'sweet', naming the starchy bulb of a blue-flowered lily prized as a food staple. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Chinook Jargon as kamas. Spread into the Pacific Northwest trade language among many indigenous groups. It reached English by 1805 as camas. Recorded by the Lewis and Clark expedition after being fed camas bulbs by the Nez Perce. Today, camas means a blue-flowered lily of western North America with an edible bulb, historically a staple food.
