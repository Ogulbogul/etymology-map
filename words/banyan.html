Read the full story

Banyan’s story begins in Sanskrit: vanij meant “a merchant.” Meant 'merchant, trader'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Gujarati as vaniyo. Named the Gujarati merchant caste. From there it passed into Arabic as baniyan by medieval. Arab traders adopted the word for the Hindu merchants they dealt with. Portuguese picked it up next, as baniano, around 16th c.. Portuguese traders at Persian Gulf ports used the term. It reached English by 1630s as banyan. Applied first to the merchants, then to the fig tree they traded and sheltered beneath at a Gulf port. Today, banyan means a large fig tree native to India that grows aerial roots from its branches, forming multiple trunks.
