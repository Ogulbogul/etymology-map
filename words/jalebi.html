Read the full story

Jalebi’s story begins in Arabic: zalābiya meant “fried sweet.” Described in al-Baghdadi's cookbook of 1226. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as zulbiya. A fritter soaked in syrup, still made for Ramadan in Iran. From there it passed into Hindi as jalebī by 1400s. Spread through India with Persian cooking. It reached English by 1900s as jalebi. Borrowed with Indian sweets. Today, jalebi means a South Asian sweet of fried batter spirals soaked in syrup.
