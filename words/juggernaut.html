Juggernaut

4 stages from Sanskrit to English.

Stage of in this word's full journey.

Today it means a huge, powerful force or institution that crushes everything in its path

Where does the word "juggernaut" come from? It comes from Sanskrit jagat + natha, passing through Hindi and English before entering English (1854).

jagat + natha→Jagannath→Juggernaut→juggernaut
1
Stage 1 of 4
jagat + natha
Sanskrit · ancient
"lord of the world"
combining jagat (the world, that which moves) and natha-s (lord, master)
2
Stage 2 of 4
Jagannath
Hindi · medieval
"Lord of the World, the deity Jagannath"
the name of a form of the Hindu deity Krishna/Vishnu, worshipped at a great temple in Puri, Odisha
3
Stage 3 of 4
Juggernaut
English · 1630s
"the massive temple cart of Jagannath"
British travelers' accounts described the deity's massive temple cart, wrongly said to crush devotees beneath its wheels
4
Stage 4 of 4
juggernaut
English · 1854
"an overwhelming, unstoppable force"
generalized to mean any idea, force, or institution that demands blind devotion or crushes everything in its path
