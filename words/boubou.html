Read the full story

Boubou’s story begins in Wolof: mbubb meant “a loose, flowing robe.” Named the loose, ankle-length robe worn across the Sahel. By 1800s-1900s, the word had taken hold in French as boubou. Respelled in French as colonial Senegal adopted the Wolof term for the garment. It reached English by 1900s as boubou. Entered English fashion vocabulary describing the same flowing West African garment. Today, boubou means a wide, flowing robe or caftan worn across West Africa, especially in Senegal.
