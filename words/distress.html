Read the full story

Distress’s story begins in Latin: distringere meant “to draw apart, to afflict.” To draw apart, to afflict, from dis- (apart) and stringere (to draw tight). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as destresse. Carried into French for great suffering or hardship. It reached English by 1200s as distress. Settled as the English word for extreme anxiety, sorrow, or danger. Today, distress means extreme anxiety, sorrow, or suffering, or a state of danger or difficulty.
