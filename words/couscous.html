Read the full story

Couscous’s story begins in Berber: kuskus meant “steamed crushed wheat grains.” The name for small steamed grains of crushed durum wheat. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as couscous. From Berber 'seksu'; French colonial rule in Algeria popularized it in France. It reached English by 1600s as couscous. First recorded in English around 1593; the Arabic form is often linked to 'kaskasa', to pound. Today, couscous means a North African dish made from small steamed grains of crushed durum wheat.
