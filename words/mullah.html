Read the full story

Mullah’s story begins in Arabic: mawla meant “master, patron.” From waliya, "he governed"; originally meant "master". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as molla / mulla. Narrowed to a title for a learned religious scholar as it spread through Persian and Turkish courts. It reached English by 1610s as mullah. Adopted via Ottoman and Persian contacts. Today, mullah means a Muslim trained in Islamic theology and sacred law, often used as a title for a religious teacher or leader.
