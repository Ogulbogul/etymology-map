Read the full story

Suite’s story begins in Latin: sequi meant “to follow.” The root of 'suit', 'pursue' and 'sequel'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Vulgar Latin as secuta. A following, a set of things that go together, from sequi. From there it passed into French as suite by 1600s. Kept its French spelling for a connected set of rooms or things. It reached English by 1670s as suite. Bach's cello suites are among the best-known musical suites. Today, suite means a set of connected rooms, especially in a hotel, or a set of related musical pieces.
