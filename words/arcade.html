Read the full story

Arcade’s story begins in Latin: arcus meant “a bow, arch.” A plain word for a bow or arch. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as arcata. Named an arch, especially of a bridge. From there it passed into French as arcade by 1640s. Borrowed from Italian for a series of arches forming a vaulted passage. It reached English by 1731-1977 as arcade. Entered for an arched passage, later a shop-lined walkway, and eventually a venue for coin-operated games. Today, arcade means a covered passageway with arches, or a venue for coin-operated games.
