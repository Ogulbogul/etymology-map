Read the full story

Pasteurize’s story begins in Latin: pastor meant “shepherd.” 'shepherd', from pascere 'to feed'; Christian Latin also used it for a priest tending his flock. By 1864, the word had taken hold in French as Pasteur. A surname from Old French pastour 'shepherd'; Louis Pasteur showed in 1864, working on the wines of Arbois, that gentle heating stopped spoilage. It reached English by 1881 as pasteurize. Applied to milk from the 1880s; Chicago was the first US city to require milk pasteurisation, in 1908. Today, pasteurize means to heat food or drink to kill harmful microbes.
