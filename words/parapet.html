Read the full story

Parapet’s story begins in Latin: pectus meant “breast, chest.” A plain word for the chest or breast. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as petto. Kept the sense of the breast. By medieval, Italian speakers had reshaped it into parapetto. Para- 'defense' plus petto 'breast' - a wall built breast-high for protection. French picked it up next, as parapet, around 1500s. Borrowed from Italian for a fortification's breastwork. It reached English by 1580s as parapet. Adopted for a low protective wall along a roof, bridge, or rampart. Today, parapet means a low protective wall along the edge of a roof, bridge, or fortification.
