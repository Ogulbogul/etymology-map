Read the full story

Bouillon’s story begins in Latin: bullire meant “to bubble, boil.” The verb of bubbling or boiling. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as bolir. Kept the sense of boiling and bubbling up. From there it passed into French as bouillon by 1000s. A noun from the past participle of bouillir, naming the broth produced by boiling meat. It reached English by 1650s as bouillon. Borrowed for a clear meat broth. Today, bouillon means a clear broth made from boiled meat or vegetables.
