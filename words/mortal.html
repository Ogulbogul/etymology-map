Read the full story

Mortal’s story begins in Latin: mors meant “death.” The root of 'mortgage', 'murder' and 'mortify'. Within Latin, it grew into mortalis by ancient. Subject to death, from mors (death). From there it passed into Old French as mortal by medieval. Carried into French for a being subject to death. It reached English by 1300s as mortal. Settled as the English word for something subject to death. Today, mortal means subject to death, or a human being as distinct from a god.
