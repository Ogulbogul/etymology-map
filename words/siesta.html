Read the full story

Siesta’s story begins in Latin: sexta hora meant “the sixth hour, midday.” Literally "the sixth hour" (counted from dawn, i.e. midday). By medieval, the word had taken hold in Spanish as siesta. Came to mean the midday rest taken during the day's heat. It reached English by 1600s as siesta. Adopted through contact with Spanish culture. Today, siesta means a short nap taken in the afternoon, especially in hot climates.
