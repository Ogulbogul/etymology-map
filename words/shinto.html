Read the full story

Shinto’s story begins in Japanese: shindō meant “the way of the kami, or gods.” Coined using borrowed Chinese characters to distinguish the native 'way of the gods' from Buddhism. It reached English by 1600s-1800s as Shinto. Chinese-derived 'way of the gods'; the Japanese form is 'kami no michi'. Today, shinto means the indigenous animist and ancestor-venerating religion of Japan.
