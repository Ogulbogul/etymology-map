Read the full story

Delirium’s story begins in Latin: delirare meant “to deviate from a straight furrow, to rave.” To deviate from a straight furrow, to be crazy, from de- (from) and lira (furrow). Within Latin, it grew into delirium by ancient. Formed as a noun for a state of wild, confused excitement. It reached English by 1590s as delirium. Adopted directly for a disturbed state of mind involving confusion. Today, delirium means an acutely disturbed state of mind marked by confusion and restlessness.
