Read the full story

Gazette’s story begins in Venetian Italian: gazzetta meant “a small coin; a news-sheet.” The small coin that was the price of admission to hear the news read aloud. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as gazette. Adopted for a printed news publication. It reached English by 1600s as gazette. Venetian 'gazeta', a small coin, the price of a news sheet; the London Gazette began in 1665. Today, gazette means a newspaper or official journal, especially one containing public notices.
