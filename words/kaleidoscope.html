Read the full story

Kaleidoscope’s story begins in Greek: kalos meant “beautiful.” Beautiful, combined with eidos (form) and skopein (to view). It reached English by 1810s as kaleidoscope. Coined by Scottish scientist David Brewster for his new optical toy. Today, kaleidoscope means a tube of mirrors showing changing patterns of colored glass, or a constantly shifting pattern.
