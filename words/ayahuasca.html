Read the full story

Ayahuasca’s story begins in Quechua: ayawaska meant “spirit vine.” Literally 'vine of the dead/spirit' (aya spirit + waska vine), naming the psychoactive vine brew. By 1900s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as ayahuasca. Quechua 'vine of the soul', from 'aya', spirit, plus 'waska', rope. It reached English by 1960s-1970s as ayahuasca. Entered English through anthropological and later popular accounts of Amazonian ceremonies. Today, ayahuasca means a psychoactive plant brew used in traditional and modern Amazonian spiritual ceremonies.
