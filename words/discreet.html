Read the full story

Discreet’s story begins in Latin: cernere meant “to sift, to distinguish.” To sift, to distinguish, combined with dis- (apart). Within Latin, it grew into discernere by ancient. To sift apart, to discern, from dis- and cernere, with irregular past participle discretus. From there it passed into Old French as discret by medieval. Carried into French for someone careful and wise in speech. It reached English by 1300s as discreet. Settled as the English word for being careful to avoid embarrassment or attention. Today, discreet means careful and judicious in speech or behavior, avoiding unwanted attention.
