Read the full story

Cassowary’s story begins in Malay: kasuari meant “cassowary.” The bird lives in New Guinea, nearby islands such as Seram, and northern Australia; the Malay name may itself come from a Papuan language. By 1597, the word had taken hold in Dutch as kasuaris. The first Dutch expedition to the East Indies, under Cornelis de Houtman, brought a live cassowary back to Amsterdam in 1597. It reached English by 1610s as cassowary. Borrowed from Dutch; Linnaeus later, in 1758, classed it as Struthio casuarius, lumping it in with the ostrich. Today, cassowary means a large flightless bird of New Guinea and northern Australia with a bony head crest.
