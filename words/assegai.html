Read the full story

Assegai’s story begins in Berber: zaghaya meant “a spear.” A Berber word for a slender throwing spear used across North Africa. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Arabic as az-zaghayah. Arabic absorbed the Berber word along with its definite article. From there it passed into Portuguese as azagaia by 1500s. Portuguese explorers adopted the term while trading along the African coast. It reached English by 1600s as assegai. Entered English through accounts of southern African warfare, especially Zulu weaponry. Today, assegai means a slender, iron-tipped spear historically used as a stabbing or throwing weapon by southern African peoples such as the Zulu.
