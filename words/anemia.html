Read the full story

Anemia’s story begins in Greek: anaimia meant “lack of blood.” Greek, 'an-', without, plus 'haima', blood. It reached English by 1820s as anemia. The same Greek root 'haima' (blood) gave English a whole family of blood-disorder terms ending in '-emia', like leukemia and septicemia. Today, anemia means a shortage of red blood cells.
