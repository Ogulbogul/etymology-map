Read the full story

Veal’s story begins in Latin: vitellus meant “little calf.” A diminutive of 'vitulus', calf, a word possibly tied to 'vetus', a yearling. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as veel. Norman nobles ate the meat while English farmhands kept raising the 'calf'. It reached English by 1300s as veal. One of several pairs where the French word names the meat and the English word names the animal. Today, veal means the meat of a young calf.
