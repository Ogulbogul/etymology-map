Read the full story

Karakul’s story begins in Uzbek: Qorakoʻl meant “black lake (a town).” 'black lake', an oasis town near Bukhara that was the centre of the breed. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Russian as karakul. Russian traders named the prized Bukharan lambskin after the town. It reached English by 1890s as karakul. Sold in the West as 'Persian lamb' or 'astrakhan', it became a fashionable fur for coats and hats. Today, karakul means a Central Asian sheep, or the curly black fleece of its newborn lambs.
