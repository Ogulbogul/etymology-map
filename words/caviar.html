Read the full story

Caviar’s story begins in Persian: khaviyar meant “egg-bearing (fish roe).” Literally "egg-bearing": khaya "egg" + dar "bearing, having". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Turkish as khaviar. Ottoman traders controlled the Black Sea and Caspian sturgeon fisheries. From there it passed into Italian as caviaro by c. 1300s-1500s. Entered Europe via Venetian trade with the Ottomans. French picked it up next, as caviar, around 1500s. Rabelais mentioned caviar in the 1530s as a delicacy. It reached English by 1550s as caviar. Hamlet's 'caviare to the general' (1600) means a delicacy wasted on common people. Today, caviar means the salted roe of sturgeon or other fish, eaten as a delicacy.
