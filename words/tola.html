Read the full story

Tola’s story begins in Sanskrit: tula meant “weighing, balance.” Meant 'weighing, balance'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Hindi as tola. Became the name of a traditional unit of weight. It reached English by 1833 as tola. The British fixed the tola at 180 grains in 1833; gold is still sold by the tola in South Asia. Today, tola means a traditional South Asian unit of mass (about 11.7 grams), still used to measure gold and silver.
