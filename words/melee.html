Read the full story

Melee’s story begins in Latin: miscere meant “to mix.” The basic verb of mixing. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as mesler. Kept the sense of mixing and mingling. By 1200s, Old French speakers had reshaped it into meslee. The feminine past participle named a chaotic mixing of combatants. It reached English by 1640s as melee. Borrowed (a second time, after an earlier loan had faded) for confused hand-to-hand combat. Today, melee means a confused, hand-to-hand fight among several people.
