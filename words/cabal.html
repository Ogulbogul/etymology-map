Read the full story

Cabal’s story begins in Hebrew: qabbalah meant “a received tradition, mystical teaching.” Literally tradition or received knowledge, referring to Jewish mystical teaching. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in French as cabale. Broadened in French to mean any secret intrigue or scheme. It reached English by 1600s as cabal. Charles II's ministry of 1667–73 was nicknamed the 'Cabal' because its members' initials (Clifford, Arlington, Buckingham, Ashley, Lauderdale) spelled the word. Today, cabal means a secret group of people plotting together, especially in politics.
