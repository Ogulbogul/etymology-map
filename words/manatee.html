Read the full story

Manatee’s story begins in Taino: manatí meant “a large aquatic mammal with a paddle-shaped tail.” Named the large aquatic mammal seen in Caribbean waters. By 1550s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as manatí. Adopted by Spanish explorers describing the animal in their chronicles. It reached English by 1550s-1600s as manatee. Anglicized from Spanish accounts of Caribbean wildlife. Today, manatee means a large, slow-moving aquatic mammal found in warm coastal waters and rivers.
