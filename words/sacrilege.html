Read the full story

Sacrilege’s story begins in Latin: sacer meant “holy.” Holy, combined with legere (to gather, to steal). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as sacrilegium. Theft of sacred things, from sacer and legere. From there it passed into Old French as sacrilege by medieval. Carried into French for a violation of something sacred. It reached English by 1200s as sacrilege. Settled as the English word for the violation of something regarded as sacred. Today, sacrilege means a violation or disrespectful treatment of something sacred or highly valued.
