Serendipity

4 stages from Sanskrit to English.

Stage of in this word's full journey.

Today it means The occurrence of pleasant or valuable discoveries by accident; a fortunate, unplanned finding.

Where does the word "serendipity" come from? It comes from Sanskrit Simhaladvipa, passing through Arabic and English before entering English (1754).

Simhaladvipa→Sarandib→Serendip→serendipity

Stage 1 of 4
Simhaladvipa
Sanskrit · ancient
"an old name for the island of Sri Lanka"
meant 'Dwelling-Place-of-Lions Island', an old name for the island now called Sri Lanka

Stage 2 of 4
Sarandib
Arabic · medieval
"Ceylon, Sri Lanka"
Arab traders adopted the Sanskrit place name for the island
3
Stage 3 of 4
Serendip
English · 1708
"an old poetic name for Ceylon"
the old name entered English via Persian tales and travel writing
4
Stage 4 of 4
serendipity
English · 1754
"the faculty of making fortunate, unexpected discoveries"
coined by Horace Walpole from the fairy tale 'The Three Princes of Serendip', whose heroes made lucky discoveries by accident
