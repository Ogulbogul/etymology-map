Read the full story

Toast’s story begins in Latin: tostus meant “parched, roasted.” Past participle of 'torrere', to parch, the root of 'torrid' and 'torrent'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as toster. A verb for grilling or browning over a fire. It reached English by 1700s as toast. Spiced toast was dropped into wine, so 'drinking a toast' to someone treated them as what flavoured the drink. Today, toast means sliced bread browned by heat; a salute made by raising glasses.
