Read the full story

Rebec’s story begins in Arabic: rabab meant “bowed stringed instrument.” Named a bowed stringed instrument. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Old French as ribabe. Carried the instrument into medieval European music. By 1400s, Old French speakers had reshaped it into rebec. Reshaped, perhaps influenced by "bec" (beak), for the instrument's pointed shape. It reached English by early 1400s as rebec. Entered English for the small medieval three-stringed fiddle. Today, rebec means a small medieval three-stringed musical instrument with a pear-shaped body, played with a bow.
