Read the full story

Magazine’s story begins in Arabic: makhāzin meant “storehouses.” The plural of "makhzan", a storeroom. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as magazzino. Arabic 'makhāzin', storehouses; the publication sense began with 'The Gentleman's Magazine' of 1731. From there it passed into French as magasin by c. 1500s. Kept the sense of a storehouse or shop. It reached English by c. 1730s as magazine. Extended metaphorically to a "storehouse" of information in print. Today, magazine means a periodical publication containing articles, images, and advertising.
