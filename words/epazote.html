Read the full story

Epazote’s story begins in Nahuatl: epazotl meant “a strong-smelling cooking and medicinal herb.” Named for the pungent-smelling herb (epatl skunk + tzotl sweat/dirt). By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as epazote. Adopted into Mexican Spanish with the Nahuatl name intact. It reached English by 1920s as epazote. Entered English through Mexican cookbooks and Southwestern cuisine. Today, epazote means a pungent Mexican culinary herb traditionally cooked with black beans.
