Read the full story

Bento’s story begins in Japanese: bento meant “a boxed meal.” A single-portion boxed meal. It reached English by 1900s as bento. Richard Cocks, the East India Company's merchant at Hirado, used it in his diary as early as 1616, though it only became common in English in the 1900s. Today, bento means a single-portion boxed meal, typically Japanese, with compartments for different foods.
