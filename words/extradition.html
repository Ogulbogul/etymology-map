Read the full story

Extradition’s story begins in Latin: ex traditio meant “a handing over.” Literally a handing over from, from ex- (out) and tradere (to hand over). By 1800s, the word had taken hold in French as extradition. Coined in French for the formal surrender of a fugitive to another jurisdiction. It reached English by 1830s as extradition. The first English use followed treaties that let states hand over fugitives. Today, extradition means the surrender of a person accused of a crime to the jurisdiction where it occurred.
