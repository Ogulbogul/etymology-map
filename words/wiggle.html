Read the full story

Wiggle’s story begins in Dutch: wigelen meant “to rock, sway back and forth.” A repeated-action form of 'wiegen', to rock, itself from 'wiege' (cradle). By c. 1200, the word had taken hold in Middle English as wiglen. Borrowed for unsteady, rocking motion. It reached English by 1816 onward as wiggle. Settled into its modern spelling once used as a noun for the motion itself. Today, wiggle means to move with small quick side-to-side movements.
