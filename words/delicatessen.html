Read the full story

Delicatessen’s story begins in Latin: delicatus meant “delicate, pleasing.” Meaning "delicate" or "pleasing". By 1600s, the word had taken hold in French as délicatesse. Came to mean a delicacy or fine food. From there it passed into German as Delikatessen by 1800s. From French 'délicatesse', delicacy. It reached English by 1800s as delicatessen. German and Jewish immigrants opened delis in New York; Katz's dates from 1888. Today, delicatessen means a shop selling prepared foods, cold cuts, and delicacies.
