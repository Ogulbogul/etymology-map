Read the full story

Oaf’s story begins in Old Norse: álfr meant “elf.” An elf, one of the supernatural beings of Norse mythology. By 1620s, the word had taken hold in English as auf. Used for a changeling, the misshapen or dim child fairies were believed to leave in place of a stolen baby. It reached English by 1630s as oaf. The changeling's supposed dullness gave the modern sense. Today, oaf means a stupid, uncultured, or clumsy person.
