Read the full story

Ibis’s story begins in Egyptian: hib meant “the sacred ibis bird.” Named the sacred wading bird associated with the god Thoth. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Ancient Greek as ibis. The ibis was sacred to Thoth, the Egyptian god of writing; millions of ibises were mummified. From there it passed into Latin as ibis by ancient. Carried into Latin natural histories such as Pliny's. It reached English by late 1300s as ibis. Entered English through Latin natural-history texts. Today, ibis means a long-legged wading bird with a curved bill; several species are found worldwide, and it was sacred in ancient Egypt.
