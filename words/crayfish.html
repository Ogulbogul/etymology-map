Read the full story

Crayfish’s story begins in Frankish: *krebitja meant “crab.” A Germanic crab word, related to German Krebs 'crab'. By 1200s, the word had taken hold in Old French as crevice. A freshwater crustacean; modern French crevette 'shrimp' comes from the same word. It reached English by 1500s as crayfish. Middle English crevis was reshaped as if it contained 'fish', although the animal is a crustacean. Today, crayfish means a freshwater crustacean resembling a small lobster.
