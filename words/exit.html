Exit’s story begins in Latin: exire meant “to go out.” To go out, from ex- (out) and ire (to go). Within Latin, it grew into exit by ancient. He or she goes out, a stage direction marking a character's departure. It reached English by 1530s as exit. Settled as the English word for a way out or the act of leaving. Today, exit means a way out of a place, or the act of leaving.
