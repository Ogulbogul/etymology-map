Read the full story

Heart’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: hertǭ meant “heart.” Cognate with Latin 'cor, cordis', as in 'cordial', and Greek 'kardia'. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as heorte. In Old English poetry the seat of courage, thought and feeling. It reached English by 1500s as heart. 'by heart', from memory, dates from the late 1300s. Today, heart means the organ that pumps blood; the centre of feeling.
