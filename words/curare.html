Read the full story

Curare’s story begins in Cariban: kurari meant “an arrow poison.” Named the paralyzing plant poison used on hunting arrows. By 1670s, the word had taken hold in Spanish/Portuguese as curare. Adopted by European explorers documenting Amazonian hunting practices. It reached English by 1670s-1800s as curare. Entered English through scientific accounts, later used in medicine as a muscle relaxant. Today, curare means a toxic plant extract that causes paralysis, historically used as an arrow poison and later in medicine as a muscle relaxant.
