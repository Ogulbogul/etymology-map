Read the full story

Mehter’s story begins in Persian: mihtar meant “chief.” 'greater, chief', a title for senior servants of a court. By 1300s-1800s, the word had taken hold in Ottoman Turkish as mehter. The Janissaries' band played on the march and in battle; its sound inspired European 'Turkish music', such as Mozart's 'Rondo alla Turca' of 1783. It reached English by 1900s as mehter. Used by music historians for the Janissary band and its influence on Western composers. Today, mehter means the Ottoman military band of drums, cymbals and shawms.
