Read the full story

Dean’s story begins in Late Latin: decanus meant “leader of ten.” From 'decem', ten: a leader of ten soldiers or monks. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as deien. Head of a cathedral chapter. It reached English by 1300s as dean. University deans date from the 1500s. Today, dean means a senior church or university official.
