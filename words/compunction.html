Read the full story

Compunction’s story begins in Latin: pungere meant “to prick, to pierce.” To prick, to pierce, combined with com- (thoroughly). Within Latin, it grew into compungere by ancient. To prick sharply, to sting the conscience, from com- and pungere. From there it passed into Late Latin as compunctio by medieval. A pricking of conscience, remorse, from compungere. It reached English by 1300s as compunction. Settled as the English word for a feeling of guilt about one's actions. Today, compunction means a feeling of guilt or moral unease about doing something.
