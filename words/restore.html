Read the full story

Restore’s story begins in Latin: stare meant “to stand.” To stand, root behind instaurare (to renew, set up again). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as restaurare. To rebuild, to renew, from re- (again) and staurare (to erect, related to stare). From there it passed into Old French as restorer by medieval. Carried into French for bringing something back to its former condition. It reached English by 1300s as restore. Settled as the English word for bringing something back to a former state. Today, restore means to bring something back to its former condition, state, or position.
