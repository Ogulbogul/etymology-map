Read the full story

Bata’s story begins in Yoruba: bàtá meant “a sacred double-headed drum.” Named the hourglass-shaped, double-headed drum used in worship of Sàngó and other orisha. By 1800s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as batá. Carried to Cuba by enslaved Yoruba people and kept as a central instrument of Santería ritual. It reached English by 1900s as bata. Entered English through ethnomusicology and writing on Santería and Afro-Cuban music. Today, bata means a double-headed, hourglass-shaped drum of Yoruba origin, central to Santería ritual music.
