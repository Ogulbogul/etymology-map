Read the full story

Pity’s story begins in Latin: pietas meant “dutifulness.” Dutiful devotion to gods, country and family; Virgil's hero is 'pius Aeneas'. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as pitié. Narrowed to compassion; the same Latin word, borrowed again, gave 'piety', and Italian gave the Pietà. It reached English by 1200s as pity. Shakespeare's Othello (c. 1603) gives the word its most famous line: 'But yet the pity of it, Iago! O Iago, the pity of it, Iago!'. Today, pity means sorrow and compassion caused by others' suffering.
