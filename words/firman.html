Read the full story

Firman’s story begins in Old Persian: khvadata- meant “lord, master.” Compound meaning roughly "created by oneself," i.e. "lord". By medieval, the word had taken hold in Persian as farman. Developed into the word for a royal decree or order. From there it passed into Ottoman Turkish as ferman by medieval. Adopted for sultanic edicts across the Ottoman Empire. It reached English by 1610s-20s as firman. Entered English through accounts of Ottoman and Persian administration. Today, firman means a royal decree or mandate issued by a sovereign, especially in the former Ottoman and Persian empires.
