Read the full story

Noon’s story begins in Latin: nona hora meant “ninth hour, about 3 pm.” 'the ninth hour' counted from sunrise, about 3 pm; the monastic office of None was said then. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old English as nōn. Borrowed through the Church for the mid-afternoon service and meal. It reached English by 1300s as noon. Shifted to midday between the 1100s and 1300s; one explanation is that fasts ended at None, which gave an incentive to say it earlier. Today, noon means twelve o'clock in the day; midday.
