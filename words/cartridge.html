Read the full story

Cartridge’s story begins in Latin: charta meant “paper, leaf of papyrus.” A general word for a leaf of paper. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Italian as cartoccio. An augmentative of carta coined for a twist of paper wrapping something. From there it passed into French as cartouche by 1500s. Borrowed for a paper-wrapped charge of gunpowder. It reached English by 1570s as cartridge. The French form was garbled in English and later extended to any small pre-packaged charge or container. Today, cartridge means a container holding ammunition, ink, or other material for insertion into a device.
