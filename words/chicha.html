Read the full story

Chicha’s story begins in Kuna: chichab meant “maize.” Meant 'maize', the base ingredient of a fermented beverage made by peoples of Panama and Colombia. By 1500s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as chicha. Adopted broadly by Spanish colonists as a generic term for indigenous fermented beverages. It reached English by 1600s-1900s as chicha. Borrowed into English through travel and anthropological writing on Latin America. Today, chicha means a traditional fermented (or unfermented) beverage made from maize or fruit, popular across Latin America.
