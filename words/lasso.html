Read the full story

Lasso’s story begins in Latin: laqueus meant “noose, snare.” By medieval, the word had taken hold in Spanish as lazo. Kept the meaning of a rope loop or snare. It reached English by 1800s as lasso. From Spanish 'lazo', noose; vaqueros taught roping to American cowboys. Today, lasso means a long rope with a running noose, used to catch livestock.
