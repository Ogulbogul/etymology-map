Read the full story

Service’s story begins in Latin: servitium meant “slavery, servitude.” 'slavery, servitude', from servus 'slave'; Roman law also used servitus for rights over a neighbour's land, the source of legal 'servitudes'. By 1100s, the word had taken hold in Old French as servise. The duty a vassal owed his lord, such as the customary forty days of knight service each year. It reached English by 1100s as service. 'civil service' was first used of the East India Company's administrators, and Britain's modern civil service grew from the Northcote-Trevelyan Report of 1854. Today, service means the action of helping or doing work for someone; a system supplying a public need.
