Read the full story

Biro’s story begins in Hungarian: Bíró meant “a family name.” Surname meaning 'judge'; the Budapest journalist László Bíró patented a ballpoint pen in 1938 with his brother György, using quick-drying newspaper ink. By 1943, the word had taken hold in Argentine Spanish as birome. Bíró fled to Argentina and relaunched the pen in 1943; Argentines still call a ballpoint birome, from Bíró plus his partner Juan Jorge Meyne. It reached English by 1940s as biro. The RAF bought the pens because they didn't leak at altitude; in the 1950s Marcel Bich bought the patent rights and launched the Bic. Today, biro means (British) a ballpoint pen.
