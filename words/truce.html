Read the full story

Truce’s story begins in Frankish: treuwa meant “faith, pledge.” A Frankish word related to 'true' and 'trust'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Old French as trieve. The medieval 'Truce of God' limited fighting. It reached English by 1200s as truce. Formed in English as a plural of 'trewe'. Today, truce means an agreement to stop fighting.
