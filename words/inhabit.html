Read the full story

Inhabit’s story begins in Latin: habere meant “to have.” To have, giving rise to habitare (to dwell, frequentative of habere). By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as inhabitare. To dwell in, from in- and habitare. It reached English by 1400s as inhabit. Settled as the English word for living in or occupying a place. Today, inhabit means to live in or occupy a particular place.
