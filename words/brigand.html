Read the full story

Brigand’s story begins in Italian: brigare meant “to brawl, fight.” A verb meaning to brawl or fight, of disputed deeper origin. Within Italian, it grew into brigante by medieval. Named a lightly armed foot soldier or skirmisher. From there it passed into Old French as brigand by 1300s. From Italian 'brigante', a member of a band of foot soldiers, who often turned to plunder. It reached English by 1400 as brigand. Shifted from soldier to freebooter, as armed troops and outright criminals blurred together. Today, brigand means a bandit, especially one of a gang operating in wild or remote country.
