Read the full story

Ao dai’s story begins in Vietnamese: áo dài meant “a long, fitted tunic worn over trousers.” Literally 'long shirt', a fitted tunic worn over trousers that evolved into Vietnam's national dress. It reached English by mid-1900s as ao dai. The modern fitted form was designed in the 1930s by the Hanoi artist Cát Tường; English speakers came to know it through reporting from Saigon during the Vietnam War. Today, ao dai means the traditional Vietnamese national dress, a fitted tunic worn over loose trousers.
