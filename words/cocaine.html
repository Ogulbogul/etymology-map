Read the full story

Cocaine’s story begins in Quechua: kuka meant “the coca plant.” Named the coca shrub whose leaves were chewed for stimulant effect. By 1570s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as coca. Adopted by Spanish colonists in the Andes for the same plant. From there it passed into German as cocaine by 1856. Coined by chemist Albert Niemann, who isolated the alkaloid and added the scientific suffix '-ine'. It reached English by 1870s as cocaine. Adopted directly from the German scientific coinage. Today, cocaine means a powerful and addictive stimulant drug derived from the coca plant.
