Read the full story

Lariat’s story begins in Spanish: reatar meant “to retie.” To tie again, from Latin 're-' and 'aptāre', to fit. By 1700s, the word had taken hold in Mexican Spanish as la reata. 'the rope', the vaquero's rope for catching cattle. It reached English by 1830s as lariat. 'la reata' was run together into one word. Today, lariat means a rope used as a lasso or for tethering.
