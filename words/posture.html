Read the full story

Posture’s story begins in Latin: ponere meant “to place, to put.” To place, to put, with irregular past participle positus. Within Latin, it grew into positura by ancient. A placing, a position of the body, from ponere. From there it passed into Italian as postura by medieval. Settled in Italian for the position or bearing of the body, from Latin positura. French picked it up next, as posture, around 1500s. Carried into French from Italian postura for the way the body is held. It reached English by 1600s as posture. Settled as the English word for the position in which someone holds their body. Today, posture means the position in which someone holds their body, or an attitude toward a situation.
