Read the full story

Taxi’s story begins in Latin: taxare meant “to assess.” 'to assess, value', perhaps from tangere 'to touch'; the same verb gives English 'tax'. By 1891, the word had taken hold in German as Taxameter. From Medieval Latin taxa 'charge' + Greek metron 'measure'; Wilhelm Bruhn built the fare meter in 1891, and the first motor taxi, a Daimler Victoria with a meter, ran in Stuttgart in 1897. From there it passed into American English as taxicab by 1907. Taximeter + cabriolet; Harry N. Allen launched a fleet of meter-fitted French-built cabs in New York in 1907. It reached English by 1907 as taxi. Clipped from taxicab; London's black cabs had to pass 'the Knowledge' of city streets, a test set up in 1865. Today, taxi means a car with a driver that carries passengers for a fare.
