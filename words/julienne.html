Read the full story

Julienne’s story begins in Latin: Iulianus meant “a Roman personal name.” A personal name derived from Iulius (Julius). By medieval, the word had taken hold in French as Julien. The French form of the name, borne by an otherwise unrecorded chef. By 1700s, French speakers had reshaped it into potage a la Julienne. Literally 'soup in Julien's manner', naming a clear soup of finely cut vegetables. It reached English by 1759-1889 as julienne. Entered for the soup, then narrowed to the technique of cutting vegetables into thin strips. Today, julienne means vegetables or other food cut into long, thin strips.
