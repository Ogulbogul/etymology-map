Read the full story

Mouse’s story begins in Proto-Germanic: mūs meant “mouse.” Cognate with Latin 'mūs' and Greek 'mys', one of the oldest shared animal words. By 700s-1000s, the word had taken hold in Old English as mūs. The plural 'mȳs' gave 'mice'. It reached English by 1500s as mouse. The computer mouse was named in the 1960s at the Stanford Research Institute for its tail-like cable. Today, mouse means a small rodent with a long tail.
