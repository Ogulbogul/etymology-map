Read the full story

Dhurrie’s story begins in Sanskrit: dalita meant “split, divided (as cane or mat).” Meant 'split, divided', from a root meaning 'to split'. By medieval, the word had taken hold in Hindi as dari. Became the name of a flat-woven cotton rug. It reached English by 1875 as dhurrie. British traders adopted the word for the handwoven Indian rug. Today, dhurrie means a thick, flat-woven cotton rug traditionally made in India.
