Read the full story

Quiet’s story begins in Latin: quies meant “rest, repose.” Rest, repose. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as quietus. At rest, calm, from quies. From there it passed into Old French as quiete by medieval. Carried into French for a state of calm and stillness. It reached English by 1300s as quiet. Settled as the English word for making little or no noise. Today, quiet means making little or no noise, or a state of calm and peace.
