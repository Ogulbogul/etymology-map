Read the full story

Cherimoya’s story begins in Quechua: chirimuya meant “cold seeds.” Combined 'chiri' (cold) and 'muya' (seeds), describing the high-altitude fruit tree. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Spanish as chirimoya. Quechua 'cold seeds', since the tree grows in cool Andean highlands. It reached English by 1750s as cherimoya. Reached English through reports of the French scientific expedition to Peru led by Pierre Bouguer and La Condamine in the 1730s and 1740s. Today, cherimoya means a sweet, custard-textured Andean fruit sometimes called the 'custard apple'.
