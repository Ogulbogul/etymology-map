Read the full story

Ration’s story begins in Latin: reri meant “to reckon, calculate.” A verb meaning to reckon or calculate. Within Latin, it grew into rationem by ancient. A noun for reckoning that later, in Medieval Latin, specialized to a computed food allowance. From there it passed into French as ration by medieval. Narrowed to a fixed allotment of food or supplies. It reached English by 1550-1702 as ration. Entered meaning 'reasoning', then specialized to a fixed food allowance, especially in military and wartime use. Today, ration means a fixed allowance of food or provisions, especially during scarcity.
