Read the full story

Dolman’s story begins in Ottoman Turkish: dolama meant “long robe.” A long robe worn by the Janissaries, from 'dolamak', to wind around. By 1600s, the word had taken hold in Hungarian as dolmány. Hungarian hussars made it a short braided jacket, and hussar uniform spread across European armies. From there it passed into German as Dolman by 1700s. Adopted with the hussar regiments of Prussia and Austria. It reached English by 1580s as dolman. First a Turkish robe; 'dolman sleeves', cut deep from the waist, date from 1930s fashion. Today, dolman means a long Turkish robe; a hussar's braided jacket; a loose cape-like women's garment.
