Read the full story

Car’s story begins in Gaulish: karros meant “wagon.” A Celtic word for a wagon; Caesar's Gallic War describes the Helvetii gathering carri (baggage carts) for their migration in 58 BC. By classical, the word had taken hold in Latin as carrus. Romans borrowed the Gaulish word for a four-wheeled baggage wagon; it is also the root of 'carry', 'chariot' and 'career'. From there it passed into Old North French as carre by 1000s. The Norman dialect kept the hard k- (central French has char) and brought the word to England after 1066. It reached English by 1300s as car. Meant any wheeled vehicle or chariot; it was applied to railway carriages in the US from the 1830s and to motor cars from the 1890s. Today, car means a road vehicle with an engine, four wheels and seats for a few people.
