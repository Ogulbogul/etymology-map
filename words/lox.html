Read the full story

Lox’s story begins in Yiddish: laks meant “salmon.” Meaning "salmon", from an old Germanic root. It reached English by 1900s as lox. Yiddish 'laks', salmon; bagels and lox became a New York Sunday brunch staple. Today, lox means salmon that has been cured in a brine solution.
