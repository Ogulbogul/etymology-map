Read the full story

Deus ex machina’s story begins in Greek: theos ek mekhanes meant “god from the machine.” God from the machine, describing a stage device that lowered actors playing gods into a play. By ancient, the word had taken hold in Latin as deus ex machina. Translated from Greek theatrical vocabulary for the same stage device. It reached English by 1690s as deus ex machina. Adopted into English literary criticism for a contrived plot resolution. Today, deus ex machina means an unexpected, artificial plot device that suddenly resolves a story's difficulties.
