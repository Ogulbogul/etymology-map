Read the full story

Aggregate’s story begins in Latin: grex meant “flock, herd.” The root of 'gregarious', 'congregate' and 'egregious'. Within Latin, it grew into aggregare by ancient. To bring together into a flock, from ad- (to) and grex (flock). It reached English by 1400s as aggregate. First recorded before 1425 in the writings of John Wyclif and his followers, taken from scholastic Latin. Today, aggregate means a whole formed by combining separate parts or elements.
