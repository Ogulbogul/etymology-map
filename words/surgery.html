Read the full story

Surgery’s story begins in Greek: kheirourgía meant “manual work, surgery.” 'handwork', kheir 'hand' + ergon 'work'; the Hippocratic treatises On Fractures and On Joints set out surgical techniques. By classical, the word had taken hold in Latin as chirurgia. Borrowed from Greek; Celsus's De Medicina (c. AD 30) devotes its last books to surgery. From there it passed into Anglo-Norman as surgerie by 1200s. Worn down from Old French cirurgie. It reached English by 1300s as surgery. Henry VIII's charter of 1540 united London's barbers and surgeons in one company; British doctors' 'surgery' hours come from the same word. Today, surgery means the treatment of injuries or disease by cutting open the body.
